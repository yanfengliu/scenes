## The post chain

`window.__scene.describe().post` reports what it settled on, and since 2026-09-17 it carries the watchdog's counters with it — `watch.floor` alone cannot answer "did the watchdog fire", because a ratchet followed by a re-tune back to an allowed rung leaves the floor where it was.

**The watchdog's nine black pixels are a trigger and not a verdict**, since 2026-09-17: reading the default framebuffer back is not reliable (`tools/record.js` has said so for months), and one such read was ratcheting the chain down permanently in **1 of 25 fresh `npm run shot` runs on this machine**, drawing `1f74009907c0` where the contract is `e95a53185ee3`. A black reading now runs `verifyComposer` at the current configuration — which reads the composer's own targets and never the default framebuffer — and re-reads the nine pixels after it; only a bad target or a canvas that is still black steps down. A contradicted reading is counted in `watch.falseAlarms` and announced with `console.log`, NOT `console.warn`, because `shot` fails on a `post:` warning and a warning about a frame that turned out to be fine would move the defect rather than remove it. After three CONTRADICTED readings the watchdog switches itself off for that page and says so — and note what that does not cover, because the red proofs show it: a read that comes back black EVERY time is never contradicted, so it never reaches the give-up path and the chain walks the whole ladder down, which is the right answer for a canvas that is black and the wrong one for a driver that cannot be read. All five paths are red-proved by forcing the probe's answer and the verification's verdict (`docs/learning/gate-proofs.md`), and the field rate (1 of 25 before, 0 of 25 after) is reported there as too small to measure the fix — what carries it is the forced arm.

**And the watchdog’s `renderer.info` restore leaves `frame` alone.** That field is not a statistic, it is three’s once-per-frame memo key for geometry, video-texture and UBO uploads, so restoring it wholesale rolled the counter back and could let the next few real frames skip an upload in silence. Found by an independent review of the same mistake written into `src/lighting.js` on 2026-09-17, and fixed in both.

**A step-down was announced and nobody was listening.** `src/post.js` says so with `console.warn` and `collectErrors` only ever looked at `console.error`, so the scene could be drawn at a lesser configuration on any gate's page and every gate would report a pass and print nothing. Every gate now echoes the page's `post:` warnings as they arrive (`tools/lib/browser.js`), and `shot` fails on one: the scored frame is the full chain or it is not the contract. The same file echoes `env:` since later that day, for `src/lighting.js`’s proof of the environment map.

## The environment map

That is the hole the unexplained flake of that day fell through: removing the environment puts **416 of 528 cells on the bad frame exactly**, against 63 for the good frame and 68 for the next best of twelve scene knobs. It now measures four things after building it and reports them through `window.__scene.describe().env`: the radiance the map carries (mean luma **115.6** on a GPU, **117.6** on SwiftShader, **0** for a black one), the shape three compiles each program's cube-UV lookup from, which environment each lit program was built against (239 of 256 standard materials have a program at the photo view and all 239 agree; three writes that field only when it builds a program and never tests it for invalidation, so it is a cheap direct read whose natural case is covered by the identity check beside it), and **what the map actually adds to the photo view** at 64x64 with no post chain — 2.69 luma levels on SwiftShader and 2.64 on the GPU, against a floor of `max(0.25, 4 x RIG.envIntensity)`. That last one is the end-to-end number, and it is the same size as the 2.9 that separates the flake's frame from the contract's.

**The contribution is measured through `scene.environmentIntensity` and never through `scene.environment`**, measured rather than assumed: the intensity is a uniform and costs 9 ms on a GPU and 428 ms on SwiftShader, while clearing the environment recompiles 18 programs and costs 1.2 s and 1.7 s, and the two answers differ by 0.02 of a luma level.

**A bad MAP is built once more** before it is installed, because the dome is deterministic and a second build is a real remedy for a transient; a map that is fine and whose light does not arrive is reported and NOT rebuilt, because rebuilding cannot fix a lookup or a binding.

**Nothing throws and nothing is switched off**: `npm run shot` refuses the frame and `npm run compare` refuses the sidecar, which is the split `src/post.js` settled on for its watchdog.

**One scene is required to carry the block, not every scene**: the proof is scene 1’s rig, a second scene has its own rig under `src/` and its own `describe()`, and demanding it everywhere made `SCENE=whitehouse npm test` an unconditional red — a scene that reports a block is checked whatever scene it is. Bounds, and they are real: the map and the contribution are measured ONCE, at build, so a map that goes black afterwards is invisible and only the cheap half — the texture's identity, its shape, the intensity and the per-material binding — is re-read at the shot, so a mechanism that loses the light AFTER the build without moving any of those four reads as proved and scores.

**The mechanism of the 2026-09-17 frame is still unknown, so nothing here says that frame would have been caught** — only that a map which fails at build now is; the shape check covers a height of **0** and a shape that CHANGES after the build, and a wrong height baked in at build is caught by nothing, measured on SwiftShader (at height 4 the lookup reads the wrong texels, the contribution reads 3.27 against that renderer's healthy 2.69, and every check passes); and `src/lighting.js` announces a failure with `console.warn('env: ...')`, which **every gate echoes** — `tools/lib/browser.js` gained `env:` beside `post:` and `watchdog:` the same day, because shipping a warning nothing listens to would have recreated, for the light, the defect this repo had already paid to find for the post chain. `shot`, `compare` and `try` refuse on the STATE; the line is what a person reads. The measurements are in `docs/devlog/detailed/2026-09-17-env-proof.md`.

## The scene's modules

The scene is assembled by `src/scene.js` from modules: `paving.js` (instanced slabs: stairs, gutter, landing, street, side stair, platform), `walls.js` (stacked stones, the low plaster wall and its tiled cap, the fence's stone core, the pots), `roofs.js` (kawara pan tiles, caps, ridges, rafters, boards, the fence's small roof), `facades.js` (board walls, lattices, shoji, balcony, doors, awning, sign, lanterns, fence boards, lamp post, noren), `architecture.js` (what is still block-out: roof undersides, the right roof mass, the paved bands; plus the dormer's body), `vegetation.js` (the cherry: spline limbs, strands, blossom cards colored from the photo field; pines; shrubs, potted plants, moss, weeds), `figures.js` (the person), `animation.js` (the wind, the petals, the drift and the sway, and the clock the gates pin), `background.js` (mountain layers, the hill surface, the far houses and their ground, the bend's block-out). `boot.js` resolves the scene and `scenes.js`/`picker.js` list and switch them, `primitives.js` holds the mesh helpers, `instancing.js` the InstancedMesh and geometry helpers, `textures.js` the canvas albedo and card generators, `materials.js` the material factory (lit since phase 4; `MATERIALS.lit` selects the material class, not a whole unlit pipeline), `tonemap.js` the tone curve and its inverse, `lighting.js` the rig, `sky.js` the dome, `post.js` the composer, `photofield.js` the baked canopy field and mask, `random.js` the seeded PRNG.
