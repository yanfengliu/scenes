## B: the frame's two worst cells for three iterations

The photo at `npm run inspect -- pair 0.75 0.86 1.0 1.0` and `crop 0.76 0.90 1.0 1.0 10` shows a stone step across the near end of the right terrace, its riser in deep shade with moss in the joints. Measured shadow depth, since the brief asks for it: the photo's riser is #14191d against #5a6570 on the slab above it, a linear ratio of 0.068, 0.075, 0.076 per channel — **about 93% of the light gone**. This rig cannot produce that and does not have to. Evaluated from `RIG` and `sunDirection()` in src/lighting.js rather than by eye — the sun's own L is (0.136, 0.216, -0.967), the fill's (0.159, 0.398, 0.903), and three weights the hemisphere by 0.5 * dot(n, up) + 0.5 — an UP-facing surface receives 0.780 ambient + 0.039 sun + 0.140 hemisphere + 0.024 fill = **0.983**, and a +z-facing one 0.780 + 0.000 + 0.120 + 0.054 = **0.954**. Turning a surface back at the camera and away from this sun removes **2.9%**; a -x-facing wall gets 0.900, which is 8.4%; and iteration 3's critic measured the deepest CAST shadow at 14%. The environment map at `envIntensity` 0.12 is not in that sum and varies with the normal too, so these are the analytic lights only. (A first draft of this paragraph said 0.998 for the up-facing case and "removes 4%" — hand arithmetic that had the sun's elevation and the hemisphere's weighting wrong. The numbers above come from a script that reads the rig's own constants.) So the riser is a SURFACE with its own sampled colour, the way `stair risers` and `sideStepFoot` are, and nothing is painted on a horizontal surface: with the drop at 0.40 m the riser's foot projects to v = 1.005, past the frame's bottom edge, so the whole bottom cell row at u 0.79 to 0.88 is the riser's own face.

## Critic

One independent critic, read-only, on the diff and the claim, asked why the numbers do not support it. It reproduced **21 of 21** photo box means I quote, byte for byte, including the whole balcony v-ladder and all six bottom-row cells, so the colour measurements are honest; every finding is about arithmetic, geometry or the record. It also checked the name rules (no mesh renamed or added; `NO_CAST`, `PLACEMENT_CHECKS`, `GROUNDING_CHECKS`, `namerules` and `clearance`'s lists all match the same populations), verified both of the riser's eulers against the geometry, verified that the rejected arms are really absent from the tree, and confirmed from pose 4 that the step reads as a step and that nothing floats.

It also caught, before its report, that **the tree on disk was no longer the tree the scores, the sweep and the suite log belonged to**: it re-hashed all 27 files the way `treecheck` does and found `src/layout.js` and `src/paving.js` diverged. It was right — a self-review pass had moved `topRidgeY` by 1.7 mm — and everything below was re-measured and re-run on one tree afterwards.

Five defects, fixed:

1. **The step riser's body was 0.0046 m deep, not 0.12.** `slabGeometry(0.03, 0.008)` spans 0.038 m in local y, and the scale component MULTIPLIES that span — every other slab in `paving.js` passes 1 for it, and the two real risers in that file use `riserGeo`, a unit box, where the component is metres. `stepRiser` takes the geometry now and divides. Consequence the critic traced through it: the `right walkway` band's step came out as a slant and **stood up to 0.076 m proud of the lower flagstones** over x 2.15 to 2.78, a dark ridge at the foot of the step from every pose but the photo's.
2. **The band's "0.15 m behind the step" offset was a branch that never fired.** `bandSolid(…, 40)` samples z at multiples of 0.3825 and both -5.05 and -5.20 fell inside the gap from -5.3550 to -4.9725, so the profile was identical with the offset and without it, at 10 samples and at 40. It is 160 samples now (a 0.0956 m gap) with the drop a whole riser depth behind the step, which puts the slant inside the riser's own body.
3. **`wallCoping`'s sampling box straddled the two populations the coping exists to separate** — this iteration's own thesis, committed by the fix for it. (0.70,0.90)-(0.78,0.945) reads #5f6a71; its left half is #485255 and its right half **#76828d**, and at v 0.9225 that box spans x 1.565 to 2.191 while the coping is built over 1.82 to 2.15. The colour is the right half now. The inner edge moved with it: 1.86 (the critic's read of the ramp's turn) is worse at both ends than 1.78 or 1.82, because a CELL spans x and an edge at 1.86 uncovers half of the two light cells the coping is for; 1.82 clears the last dark sample at 1.79 and scores with 1.78.
4. **The coping's nearest stone was laid flat across the terrace's kink.** Row 0 was centred at z = -5.493, above the bend at -5.6, where `slopeAt` reads 0, so the whole 0.87 m stone came out horizontal with its far end 0.142 m proud of the wall and a 0.119 m drop to the next — 15 px in the scored frame inside cell (0.729, 0.886). `rightWalkway` guards that case with a slope test; the coping is laid in two spans cut at the kink.
5. **`wallSkirt` moved in the same edit as the bed and was never swept on its own.** Swept now on the shipped tree with everything else held: 0.20 gives 0.05993 / 0.57065, 0.30 gives 0.05969 / 0.57093, **0.38 gives 0.05958 / 0.57134** and 0.45 gives 0.05961 / 0.57110. The critic's direction (smaller, from the ladder) is measurably worse, and 0.38 stands. Its defending ladder is restated against the SHIPPED bed in `RIGHT_BED`'s comment, because the old one was 0.45 m out.
