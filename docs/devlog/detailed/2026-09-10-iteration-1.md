## The cause behind each dark that rendered light

The brief asked which of three causes was at work per material: a box sampled across a lit and a dark region, the rig's ambient share lifting a dark albedo, or a lighter texture correction. Measured per material, by comparing the material's own constant, the render's mean over the band the mesh fills, and the photo's mean over the same band:

- **`plinth` (right house plinth), photo #2f2b28, render #5d5f5f.** Cause: **the sample box**, and specifically one box across two lightings. The material is 0x57595b = (87,89,91) and the render displays (93,95,95) over that band, so the rig is lifting it by 7% and nothing else — a 3.2x gap cannot come from that. The plinth runs the whole machiya, and the photo's base band goes from #2f2b28 at u 0.66-0.78 to #515457 at u 0.87-0.95. Darkening the single colour to fit the far half made the near half worse and both scores worse (0.0817 / 0.4372). Split at `M.baseSplitZ`, the same z the base above it already splits at: `plinth` 0x4c4f53 near, `plinthFar` 0x363432 far.
- **`eaveEdge` / `leftRoofEdge` (house 1's eave), photo #36241c at u 0.12-0.185, render #786656.** Cause: **the sample box across a lit and a dark region**, again. Sampled in thirds along the band the eave actually fills, the photo reads #ad8b6b at u 0.00-0.06, #987557 at 0.06-0.12 and #36241c at 0.12-0.185 — a 120-level ramp along 3 m of eave. The old comment in `roofs.js` recorded that "a darker underside scored worse", which is true and is exactly what a single colour on a ramp does. Split at z = −7.6, with a second correction the critic found (below): the rafters over the near half also had to be lightened, because the split is a diagonal in photo space and the cell it was aimed at is rafters, not fascia.
- **`house1Upper` (the mezzanine boards and the dormer), photo ~(36,32,30), render (66,60,56).** Cause: **both**. The material is 0x393737 = (57,55,55) and displays (74,64,56) — the rig lifts a dark albedo by about 30% here and warms it (red +30%, blue +2%), because the wall faces the camera and takes the fill. The remaining 1.85x is the sample: 0x393737 is the mean of a band that includes the sudare's lit strip below the boards. Set to 0x1f1e1e.
- **`farWallLow` (the far houses' lower walls), photo ~(143,132,113), render (92,84,74).** Cause: **the sample box**, taken at the one dark cell in the region. The old comment names cells (0.354, 0.659) and (0.313, 0.682); (0.354, 0.659) is indeed #565944, but the wall also fills (0.313, 0.659) #aaa18d, (0.313, 0.705) #b18962 and (0.354, 0.705) #8b8e92. Set to 0x776f5f.
- **`tileLeft` on the annex roof, photo #776656 / #8f6946, render #8c8d8f.** Cause: **the wrong colour reused**, not a bad box. House 1's roofs are blue-grey kawara and the annex's lean-to is warm brown in the photo; both were on `tileLeft`. New `tileAnnex` 0x947d63, used only by the annex roof.
- **`tileRight` (the right machiya's kawara), photo #8499b5, render #72798b.** Cause: **neither of the three — the geometry**. The pan tiles have an S-shaped section (`panTileGeometry`), so half of every tile turns away from the light and the plane's mean displays 15-30% below what a flat surface with the same albedo would. That is a real shading effect, so raising the albedo to compensate is legitimate; it was tried at 0x87a3c8 and 0x8fb0dc and moved the scores by less than 0.0001 either way, because the same roof spans cells that want +30% (u 0.77, v 0.386) and cells that want −13% (u 0.85, v 0.432). Left alone, with the measurement written into the comment.

## What I believed and proved false

- **That the two far-street cells at (0.438, 0.75) and (0.563, 0.75) are "the far street darkening into the bend".** They are not a shading gradient. Probed, both are `landing slabs` at 23.0 and 23.3 m — the same depth as (0.354, 0.75), which needs the opposite correction. In the photo at v 0.75 the street runs from u 0.28 to about u 0.42 and everything right of that is the dark machiya row lining the far street. The render's landing is 7 m wide: at z = -21 its right edge sits at x = 2.5 where the photo's street edge is at x = -2.0, so about 4.5 m of paving stands where the row should be, and its right edge projects to u 0.61. One set of slabs therefore answers for lit stone at u 0.35 and for buildings at u 0.44, and no colour or depth ramp can serve both. That is why every attempt on the paving lost: lifting `landing` toward the photo's own landing box (#a19d9f against the material's 0x76818d) cost 0.0005 of cell distance and 0.004 of SSIM, isolating the street slabs from the terrace bands did not help, and a deeper shade ramp was neutral. **The fix is the missing machiya row, which is iteration 2's content work.** A crude dark block-out was measured against the photo and rejected: at v 0.75 the photo's u 0.52 cell is #516371 and its u 0.44 cell is #323840, so a solid wall would trade one error for another.
- **That extending the left stone wall would cover cells (0.063, 0.568) and (0.104, 0.568).** It does — the ray at (0.063, 0.568) misses the wall's far end by 6 cm — but it scored 0.0802 / 0.4400, worse on both. The wall's far edge is a slanted line from u 0.048 at its top to 0.079 at v 0.75, and pushing it out moved that whole edge, not just the two cells. What worked was the object the photo actually shows there: a plaster band under the eave, carried along the near house front (z −4.6 to −6.7 after the critic moved both ends) the way it already runs over the annex's far end. That alone was 0.0778 → 0.0769.
- **That the balcony's floor sits too low.** Cell (0.896, 0.295) is the balcony's floor slab where the photo has cream shoji, and raising the floor from 4.9 to 5.1 does put the slab behind the shoji — and scores 0.0796 / 0.4453 against 0.0795 / 0.4465. The balcony is too *long*, not too low: in the photo it only reaches u 0.87, and the render carries it the length of the upper floor. Left for a later iteration.
- **That a whole-material colour is the right unit.** Two of the eight kept changes are geometry splits and a third is a second colour inside one of them: a material that spans a lit end and a shaded end has no correct single mean, and the per-cell ranking punishes both ends at once. The eave split costs about 5 draw calls, the plinth split 1.

## What the orbit sweep shows

`npm run views` is new: seven fixed poses to `out/views/<pose>.png` at 1200x1100 plus a labelled `out/views/index.png`. It poses the camera directly through `window.__scene` and applies the frame loop's own clamp (`clampCamera`, newly exposed on the scene API for this) — it does **not** exercise the controls; `npm run record` is the gate that does. It prints each file's sha256 and mean luminance and warns when a frame comes back black, when two poses come back byte-identical, or when the clamp corrected nothing, because none of those is a page error and all three look exactly like a run that worked.

The inspection below is of these exact bytes, on the scene at 0.0768 / 0.4559: `1-photo` 78c19bb98c17, `2-orbit-left-up` 9f0e689f5e97, `3-orbit-right-up` f0ef2afc4c1e, `4-paving-down-steps` 530b341869fe, `5-landing-look-back` 65cdb04540e3, `6-high-over-roofs` 85b79ace2066, `7-clamped-out-of-the-wall` 4562edf3ce3d, sheet f431091f83c8 (mean luminance 129.9, 111.0, 132.0, 120.8, 118.8, 116.5, 100.6). Each was looked at at its own 1200x1100, not on the contact sheet. What is wrong:

1. **photo view** — the scored framing; nothing new.
2. **orbited left and up** — the hill is a flat dark speckled band with a hard straight silhouette; the ground between the houses and the hill is a featureless pale plane; the left house row's back and sides are untextured tan slabs; the buildings are open boxes with no back walls; the canopy is a thin shell with an empty interior.
3. **orbited right and up** — the right machiya's back roof is one flat blue plane with no tile relief, no ridge and no eave; its back and side walls are bare slabs with no openings; the mountains are flat coloured strips with straight horizontal edges (the blue band reads as a mistake); the pines are sticks of cards with visible gaps and no crown.
4. **close to the paving, down the steps** — the stairs read as a smooth ramp because there is no shadow line at the nosings; the right retaining wall's stones are a visibly repeating diamond pattern of identical blocks; the right third is one flat untextured dark grey mass; the person is a flat billboard; the cherry's strands are hard black wires.
5. **from the landing, looking back up** — **a bare grey slab stands across the head of the stairs**: the raised top platform's face, which the photo view never sees because it is behind the camera. It reads as a wall closing off the street and carries the cherry's shadow like a projection screen. Also: the stone walls repeat their block texture about every metre; the treads are uniformly bright with no wear; the limbs are straight black lines against the sky. This pose also proved the `maxPolarAngle` limit of 0.58 PI is a real constraint — the first version of it asked for a steeper look-up, and `controls.update()` swung the camera back and up into the middle of the canopy.
6. **high over both roof rows** — the world is two buildings on a featureless plane with hard horizontal fog bands; both roofs are single planes meeting in a bare edge with no ridge tiles; the right machiya has a bare grey slab where its roof stops; the far houses' walls end in mid-air.
7. **asked for a pose inside the right machiya and below the street** — the clamp corrects it to the street's right edge at (1.65, −1.84, −7.58), which is the pose in the file; the eye ends 0.45 m above the paving. What it shows: the right retaining wall and walkway are one untextured dark grey mass filling half the frame at close range, with flat facets and a hard silhouette; the side stairs are a stack of plain grey blocks with no stone in them; the cherry's strands are hard black sticks. It is not a pretty view and it is not meant to be — it is the one pose here that proves the clamp ran.

Items 2, 3, 5, 6 and 7 are the block-out iteration 2 is for. Item 5's platform slab is the one defect here that nothing else could have found: it is outside the scored frame in every direction, so no score will move when it is fixed or when it breaks.

## Traps for the next session

- **Every delta above is conditional.** Each was measured on the tree as it stood, so a change kept early is not proved to still be worth its place. The plinth split is the clearest case: it was worth 0.0001 of cell distance on a tree that still had house 1's roof in the sky, which is inside the 0.0010 SSIM noise floor this session measured on unchanged code.
- **A split's boundary is a diagonal in photo space, not a line of constant u.** The eave runs almost along the line of sight, so a split at one z crosses the fascia at u 0.041 and the rafter line at u 0.087. Sampling the band in thirds of u and then splitting in z assigns the wrong colour to whichever part is furthest from the sampled axis — which is what happened here, and it took the critic to see it.
- **Adding a `tileRoof` call shifts every later roof's seed.** `tileRoof` draws `seed = 40 + floor(rand() * 1000)` from the module-level `rand` in `src/roofs.js`, so the two new calls (house 1's eave far, and nothing else) renumber every roof built after them and change their per-instance tint jitter. The scene is still deterministic, but an A/B across a change that adds or removes a roof is not comparing only that roof.
- **`C.landing` is the hemisphere light's ground tint** (`src/lighting.js`), as well as the landing slabs, the lit far-street slabs and the right terrace's paved bands. Changing it to fix the paving moves the rig under every material in the scene. This was noticed, worked around with a separate constant, and then the whole paving change was rejected on its own numbers — but the coupling is still there for whoever tries next.
- **The compare gate cannot see the top platform.** The one real defect the orbit sweep found (pose 5) sits behind the photo camera, so no score will ever move when it is fixed or when it breaks.
