## What was believed and proved false

- **The eye is 1.65 m above the head of the stairs.** The plan's camera puts the eye 1.65 m above the top landing with a 60 degree vertical FOV. Counting the photo shows about 13 shallow steps between v = 0.85 (the head of the paved ramp) and the bottom of the frame. With 0.46 m treads at the 18 degree street pitch those 13 steps fit between the two rays (34 and 43 degrees below the horizontal) only if the eye is about 4.8 m above the stair line; from 1.65 m only four steps fit. The FOV does not rescue it: a 45 degree FOV brings the eye down to 1.9 m but shrinks the figure at the landing to 0.95 m. `src/layout.js` therefore puts the eye 4.8 m up and 1.65 m above a raised top landing (`PLATFORM_Y`) at the head of the stairs. The figure in blue works out to 1.28 m tall at its depth; in the crops it reads as a crouching or small person, so that is consistent. The manager should decide whether the raised landing stays or the eye should hover.
- **"Left house 2 roof" (0.05-0.27, 0.36-0.50) and "left house 3 roof" (0.10-0.33, 0.50-0.62) are two houses.** They are the same building: a single-story annex under house 1's eave with a lean-to roof (the first box) and a door canopy stepping down along the street (the second box). Both slope down along z with the terraces and up away from the street; the canopy's far end sits at photo (0.34, 0.61) and the annex roof's eave at (0.27, 0.50). A third house with its own roof begins at z = -11.5 and is mostly hidden.
- **The left wall is one retaining wall with a plaster cap (0-0.27, 0.68-1.0).** Nearest the camera (u < 0.12) it is a tall wall of light stone blocks up to 3.7 m; the low plaster wall with the tile cap and the potted plant is a separate, much lower wall in front of the house fronts, whose cap descends steeply from (0.10, 0.84) to (0.28, 0.88) in the frame.
- **The right terrace is one level.** The pot at (0.85, 0.75) and the fence with the tile cap (0.60-0.78, 0.60-0.70) disagree about the ground height by about 1.5 m: the fence stands on a raised planter bed (shrubs behind it) 1.5 m above the walkway the pot stands on. Beyond the planter (z < -15) the walkway drops to street level and the landing widens to the right.
- **The cherry canopy is the box (0.34-0.78, 0.17-0.60).** Per-cell means show blossom out to u = 0.29 on the left (in front of the evergreen, which shows only at its top) and to u = 0.83 on the right at v 0.18-0.32, in front of the balcony. The main canopy therefore sits behind the right machiya's far end with a shallow depth radius, and two upper-right lobes are branches reaching forward to depth 11-12.
- **The right machiya's upper floor reaches u = 0.68.** The ground-floor eave does; the upper floor's balcony corner is at about u = 0.80 and the deep top eave's corner at (0.76, 0.12). Its underside is near black, only the tile ends near the camera catch the light (u > 0.80, v < 0.05), and the dark ground floor runs on to about z = -21 under the eave's far end.
- **House 1 is a tall two-story house.** It is a low-mezzanine machiya: eave roof at 4.2-4.9 m, mezzanine to 6.25 m with the sudare hanging in front, top roof at 6.65 m with sky above it and a dark underside overhanging the far end by 2.5 m.

## How placement was checked

`out/overlay.png` draws the plan's landmark boxes over a 50% blend at 1200x1100. The per-cell ranking (`out/scores.json` carries the photo and render mean colors per cell) drove the last eight rounds: each round took the worst cells, found the mesh under them with `npm run probe -- u,v`, and fixed geometry or a sampled color. `npm run inspect -- pair ...` gave photo-versus-render crops when a cell was ambiguous. The final check is the probe over every plan landmark, because the overlay alone is not one (next section).

## What a reviewer caught that the author missed

- **Two landmarks were absent from the render and the overlay hid it.** The pot at (0.85, 0.75) sat behind the raised planter strip and the right-side steps behind the retaining wall; `npm run probe` returned `right planter bed` and `right retaining wall` at every point of both boxes. The 50% overlay showed the photo's pot and steps through the blend, so "every box is met" had been read off the photo half. Fixed by narrowing the planter to 0.3 m behind the fence, cutting the wall low where the side steps rise, and re-placing both shrubs from their photo positions so the pot's ray is clear. The rule is now in `docs/policies/local-rules.md`: a placement is verified by the probe, not by the overlay.
- The cherry strands ran to v 0.68 where the plan says 0.62, and the big shrub was oversized and 0.07 high; both put back to the plan.
- `npm run shot | tail` hid a failed shot and `compare` scored the previous render. `compare` now refuses a render older than any source file (proof in `docs/learning/gate-proofs.md`), `shot` deletes its render when the page had errors, and `npm test` runs the tools without a pipe.
- The dev server crashed on `GET /%` (an uncaught `URIError`); it now answers 400. A blocked CDN left the page in `loading` until the 90 s timeout; the module script's `onerror` now rejects the handshake and `openScene` fails on the first failed script request.
- The plan asks for a landing at the foot of the stairs; the block-out had a continuous ramp. There is now a 1.5 m flat landing, with the street beyond it at 21.7 degrees so it rejoins the same line by z = -22.
- The hill's vertex-color gradient only had stops at outline vertices; it is now a grid so the stops are exact rows.
- The eye-height argument assumed the 0.15 m rise and 18 degree pitch it needed. The critic's counter-example (a 25 degree stair with 0.34 m treads from a 1.65 m eye) does fit 13 steps, but then a level landing puts the figure at 8.7 m and 0.6 m tall, and a 1.7 m figure needs the street to keep falling at 25 degrees to 24 m. Neither reading satisfies every plan number at once; the manager decides which cost to carry (see the report).
- The orbit target is on the optical axis at the cherry's depth, not at the trunk base; OrbitControls must look at its target, so the trunk base as target would change the framing. Reported as a decision.

## Environment notes

- Long heredocs (roughly 90 lines or more) fail to parse in this session's Bash tool; the Write tool is reliable for source files. Shell scripts of a few dozen lines are fine.
- Headless chromium needs `--enable-unsafe-swiftshader` for WebGL on the software renderer; `--use-angle=d3d11` gets the real GPU for perf.
- Playwright 1.61.1 was chosen because its chromium build (1228) was already in the machine's browser cache.
