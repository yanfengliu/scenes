# 2026-09-17 — `npm run try`: an iteration lane's arm on the GPU, and the renderer offset that is not a constant

An iteration lane decides everything by A/B. Move a constant, render the photo view, score it against `japan.webp`, keep the arm or discard it. The only way to ask that question was `npm run shot && npm run compare`, which is SwiftShader by design and takes no GPU switch, and which measured **93 s** in the block below. Iteration 5's devlog quotes more than thirty scored arms that survived into the writeup and says nothing about the ones that did not, so an iteration was spending on the order of an hour of wall clock waiting on a CPU rasterizer on a machine with an RTX 4090 in it.

The 2026-09-15 move put every gate whose verdict allowed it on the GPU and deliberately left `shot` and `compare` on SwiftShader. That was right: the contract has to be the same digits here and on a runner. What it did not do is give a lane a fast way to ask a question that is **not** the contract. This is that.

`tools/try.js` renders the same view at the same size with the same clock, scores it with the same two functions out of `tools/lib/metrics.js`, and does it on the GPU. It writes `out/try/render.png` and `out/try/score.json` and nothing else, so it cannot feed `compare` and cannot become the scored record.

## The thing to read first: the renderer offset is not a constant, and this repo was treating it as one

AGENTS.md has said since 2026-09-15 that "on this machine's GPU the same frame scores 0.0001 better on cell distance and 0.0009 better on SSIM". That sentence is a correct measurement of one tree and it reads as a property of the machine. The brief for this unit quoted it as one. It is not one.

Measured here on **four trees**, by moving `RIG.ambient` in `src/lighting.js` and putting it back, with the GPU arm and the software arm run back to back each time:

| tree | `RIG.ambient` | GPU minus software, cell | GPU minus software, SSIM |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2026-09-15's (recorded in AGENTS.md, 0.074947 / 0.473324 against 0.074844 / 0.474234) | — | **−0.000103** | +0.000910 |
| `dceabc6abdf61e7c`, iteration 5 as shipped | 0.78 | **−0.0000036** | +0.000894 |
| `f415aab806651a9d` | 0.76 | **−0.0000032** | +0.000852 |
| `073016e0d839fe32` | 0.68 | **+0.000069** | +0.000796 |

**SSIM's crossing is stable**: the GPU reads between 0.00080 and 0.00091 better on every tree measured, always in the same direction. **Cell distance's is not.** It is 28 times larger on the 2026-09-15 tree than on iteration 5's, and on the large-ambient arm it **changes sign** — the GPU scores the same tree *worse* than SwiftShader does. Anyone sizing a limit from the single recorded figure would be sizing it from a fact about a tree that no longer exists.

That correction is in AGENTS.md now, in two places: the renderer paragraph in Gates, and `animation`'s paragraph where the figure was quoted. `animation`'s verdict is unaffected — the cell headroom at iteration 5's score is 0.0015 (0.0611 against 0.05958), 14.8 times the largest crossing in the table, and the gate adds its own 0.0006 allowance on top for 20.6 — but the wording was an invitation to tighten something against a number that moves. **The first draft of both sentences said "more than twenty times" against the 0.0015 figure and that is false**: 20.6 is the ratio against `animation`'s own limit, 14.8 is the ratio against the threshold, and I had quoted one multiple over the other denominator. The coordinator sent it back rather than letting it stand. It is the same shape as the two retractions in iteration 5's devlog — a number stated to defend something, belonging to a different arm.

## What the tool costs, and why the number is a ratio

The machine this was measured on was at 100% across 32 logical cores, with other repositories' and other applications' work on it: by CPU seconds the top consumers were ChatGPT (43,135), a chrome (8,533), a second ChatGPT (6,512), a deepseek node process (4,662) and codex (4,277). Exactly six processes on the box were `scenes`-related.

Under that load a single absolute second means nothing, and the first four GPU runs read 12.4, 18.7, 32.7 and 15.5 s for identical work. **So the two arms were interleaved**: GPU arm, then software arm, back to back, three times, on tree `dceabc6abdf61e7c`. Two arms timed minutes apart differ by the load as well as by the renderer, which is this repo's own rule about an A/B needing the tree to hold still with load as the confound instead of the tree.

| pair | GPU arm | software arm (`TRY_GPU=0`) | ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 14 s | 85 s | 6.1 |
| 2 | 14 s | 90 s | 6.4 |
| 3 | 15 s | 88 s | 5.9 |

Median **6.1x**. Against the real baseline in the same block, `npm run shot && npm run compare` at **93 s** (shot 91, compare 2): **6.6x**. The seconds are upper bounds under a named load. The ratio is the claim.

### Where the seconds go, which is the reusable half

| phase | GPU | software |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| launch and scene build | 12.1 s | 36.9 s |
| pose and two frames | 0.0 s | 12.7 s |
| screenshot | 0.4 s | 29.8 s |
| instrument checks and two decodes | 0.2 s | 4.4 s |
| scoring | 0.1 s | 0.1 s |

**The frame work is 42.5 s of 85 on software and 0.4 s of 14 on the GPU.** That is the entire saving, and it is why the tool exists.

**And it names the next bottleneck.** On the GPU, 12.1 s of a 14 s arm is the scene BUILD — module evaluation, the three.js modules off the pinned CDN, the geometry and texture generation, and the two frames before `window.__sceneReady` settles. Every gate that opens the page pays it, about a dozen times in a suite, and this is the first measurement of it anyone here has. It is not this unit's to shorten (`src/` was outside its scope), but it is the obvious next unit: at the GPU's 12.1 s under load it is now a larger share of the suite than any rendering left in it. `page load` reads 0.0–0.1 s and the readiness wait carries the whole of it, so the split between CDN fetch, module evaluation, scene construction and the first two frames is **not yet decomposed** and should not be guessed at.

## Agreement with the contract, which is stronger than "to the digit"

The brief asked that `try` agree with `compare` to the digit when both are forced onto SwiftShader. It does better than that, and the reason is that the frame path was written to be the same path: one evaluate that appends the same `HIDE_UI_CSS` style element, calls `setTime(0)`, waits the same two animation frames and returns `describe().post` as its last act, exactly as `tools/shot.js` does.

- **The frame is byte-identical.** `TRY_GPU=0 npm run try` produced sha256 `e95a53185ee3595e` in all three software runs, which is the sha256 `npm run shot` wrote to `out/render.tree.json` in the same block and the digest AGENTS.md records for the contract.
- **The scores are equal as doubles.** `compare` read `0.05957921461787957` and `0.5713434848735502`; `try` read `0.05957921461787957` and `0.5713434848735502`. Not rounded agreement — the same bits.

So on SwiftShader this tool is not near the contract's path, it **is** the contract's path, which is why its printed margin there is zero.

## Run-to-run spread: zero, on both renderers

The margin does not come from noise, and that was measured rather than assumed, because the `referenceLuma` work the day before found a figure whose same-tree spread was 0.0 over 25 runs and it would have been easy to assume the same here without looking.

- **GPU**: every run of tree `dceabc6abdf61e7c` in this unit — 11 of them, spread across the whole session and across edits to this tool that touch no pixel path — produced one sha256, `a26790b44e18bfa0`, and one pair of doubles, `0.059575601212030586` / `0.5722374902342394`. Three are logged, under `out/scratch/base-gpu-{1,2,3}.log`; the rest are in the session transcript only.
- **SwiftShader**: 3 runs produced one sha256, `e95a53185ee3595e`, and one pair.

## The margin, and what actually bounds an A/B

A shared renderer offset **cancels** when a lane compares two arms that were both measured on the GPU. What does not cancel is the *variation* of that offset between the two trees. So the quantity to calibrate is not the offset, it is how far the GPU's delta between two arms can sit from the software delta between the same two arms.

| arms | metric | GPU delta | software delta | disagreement | as a share of the move |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| base → ambient 0.76 | cell | +0.00020761 | +0.00020718 | 0.00000043 | 0.2% |
| base → ambient 0.76 | SSIM | −0.00016817 | −0.00012586 | 0.00004231 | **34%** |
| base → ambient 0.68 | cell | +0.0042582 | +0.0041854 | 0.00007277 | 1.7% |
| base → ambient 0.68 | SSIM | −0.0021082 | −0.0020100 | 0.00009814 | 4.9% |

Note the SSIM row on the small arm: the two renderers disagree about **a third** of the move. The sign is right, the size is not transferable.

**The margin is 0.0001 on each score**, printed on every run. It is just over the largest disagreement measured (cell 0.000073, SSIM 0.000098), and large enough to cover the 0.000103 by which the cell offset itself moved between the 2026-09-15 tree and this one. Below it an arm is too close to call on the GPU and goes back to `npm run shot && npm run compare` — which is the whole reason for keeping the slow path. This tool narrows the set of arms that need 93 s down to the close ones; it never replaces them.

**A use the margin does not cover, and the tool says so on every GPU run**: never compare a `try` number against `docs/PLAN-scores.md`. Those thresholds are the software frame's, and a single GPU score carries the *whole* crossing rather than its variation — +0.0009 of SSIM on every tree measured, a fifth of the margin that file leaves. The tool answers "is arm B better than arm A", never "does this tree pass".

## It moves when the scene moves, and it comes back

`RIG.ambient` 0.78 → 0.76 moved the GPU score from `0.059575601212030586` / `0.5722374902342394` to `0.05978321167255647` / `0.572069320915191`, and the tree hash from `dceabc6abdf61e7c` to `f415aab806651a9d`. 0.78 → 0.68 moved it to `0.06383381079735596` / `0.570129330924384` on tree `073016e0d839fe32`. After the file was restored the tree hash returned to `dceabc6abdf61e7c`, `git status src/` was clean, and a fresh GPU run reproduced `a26790b44e18bfa0` bit for bit. So the tool is a function of the tree in both directions.

## That it cannot become the contract

This is the requirement the whole design turns on, because a GPU frame left where `compare` looks would be scored as the contract by the next green run and the thresholds would move onto a driver without anybody deciding to.

- `tools/try.js` has exactly three write sites: `mkdirSync('out/try')`, `page.screenshot({ path: 'out/try/render.png' })` and `writeFileSync('out/try/score.json')`. It names `out/render.png` only in prose.
- After twelve runs of it, six of them on SwiftShader, `out/render.png` still held sha256 `e95a53185ee3595e` at its original mtime, as did `out/render.tree.json` and `out/scores.json`.
- `out/try/score.json` carries a `notTheContract` field saying in the file what the log says in the terminal.

## What it refuses, and why that is not a threshold

A page error, a frame the post chain did not draw **as designed** (a step-down, a watchdog ratchet, any `post:` warning), and the scene source changing between the page opening and the screenshot. None of these is an assertion about the scene. They are instrument checks: two arms drawn at different post-chain rungs differ by the rung as well as by the constant under test, and an A/B whose tree moved between the arms is measuring more than one variable. `shot` refuses the same things for the contract's sake; this refuses them for the comparison's sake.

It also stops, rather than printing advice it cannot support, when chromium hands back the masked renderer string that names no device — the same stop `tools/animation.js` makes, because the margin is keyed on the renderer and guessing between the two rows is worse than failing.

**Both refusals were forced and seen to fire**, because a refusal that has never run is a sentence about a refusal:

- `TARGET_BYTE_BUDGET = 1` in `src/post.js` (the same mutation `shot`'s rung refusal was red-proved with): exit 1, `it was drawn at rung 0 (both the multisampling and the supersample), a 1200x1100 target at scale 1 with 0 samples, which src/post.js does not mark as the chain as designed`. Note what that catches — the rung NUMBER is 0 and the chain is still a lesser one, which is why the predicate is `designed`.
- An edit raced into `src/scenes.js` six seconds into a run: exit 1, `dceabc6abdf61e7c when the page was opened, 08e8d258749c77ff after the screenshot`, naming `src/scenes.js` and both of its digests.

The first of these also exercised the output clearing: the refused run left `out/try/render.png` from its own frame and **no** `out/try/score.json`, which is the point of clearing both before the browser opens. A lane reads those two files minutes apart, and last run's score sitting beside this run's frame is the worst thing this tool could hand one.

Both mutations were reverted, `git status src/` was clean afterwards, and the tree hash returned to `dceabc6abdf61e7c` each time.

## What was believed and proved false

- **"The GPU scores this frame 0.0001 better on cell distance."** Believed at the start of this unit, because AGENTS.md says so and the brief repeated it. It is 0.0000036 on the shipped tree and **+0.000069 the other way** on a tree with the ambient light moved. Corrected in AGENTS.md in both places it appeared.
- **"The margin is the offset."** The tool's first draft printed 0.0001 / 0.0007, the offset itself, on the reasoning that an arm moving less than the crossing cannot defend its sign. That confuses a bias with its variation: the offset cancels in an A/B and only its variation survives, and the variation was measured at 0.2% to 4.9% of the move on cell distance. The shipped margin is calibrated against the delta disagreement and against the offset's own drift between trees, not against the offset.
- **"The arm will come in under 10 s."** That target was in the brief and it was the wrong shape. Under this machine's load the arm reads 14–19 s and nearly all of it is a scene build that is not this tool's, so tuning to hit a number would have meant tuning the wrong thing. The coordinator replaced the target with "beat the software arm by a large multiple, measured interleaved" mid-unit, and that is what is reported.
- **"Run-to-run noise is what a margin is for."** Zero on both renderers here. Worth measuring rather than assuming: a margin sized for noise that does not exist would have been slack nobody could red-prove.

## Bounds

- **The calibration's sample is narrow and that is its largest bound.** One machine, one driver (ANGLE/D3D11 on an RTX 4090), and four trees that are all the *same scene* varied by *one constant in one file*. A second scene with another photograph, another frame size and another rig is outside everything measured, and so is any other GPU. Re-measure before trusting 0.0001 anywhere else; the method is three trees and two renderers, interleaved.
- It is one frame at one clock at one size, like `shot`. It says nothing about the animation, about any other camera, or about anything 528 cell means and a 64 px grayscale image cannot see.
- It scores against `japan.webp` off the working directory, which nothing in this repo binds — the same hole `tools/lib/treehash.js` already names.
- The tree hash it prints covers `index.html` and `src/**`, so two arms compared across an edit to `tools/` are not bound by it.
- The seconds in this file were all measured under the load described above and are upper bounds. Only the ratio and the phase split should be quoted elsewhere.
