## What was believed and proved false

**1. The hill's colour stops were in the wrong place, and the first draft of this entry then overstated it by 17x.** `makeHillTexture` mixes each row's stop toward the deepest stop by `glareAt(x / size)` — a Gaussian centred on `sunU`, defaulting to 0.62. `x / size` is the TEXTURE's u, and the hill texture spans photo u 0.27 to 1.6, so `sunU = 0.62` sits at photo u **1.095**, off the right edge of the frame. `sunU` and `glareWidth` are now passed in the texture's own u, converted by the caller.

What this entry first said was "over the whole visible hill the glare was about 0.01, so the three upper stops were dead constants". An independent critic measured the old Gaussian's tail from `git show a06a1c4:src/textures.js` and it is not: **0.012 at photo u 0.646, 0.052 at 0.729, 0.099 at 0.771 and 0.174 at 0.813**, a mean of about **0.081** over the six cells the hill is first under, and 0.82 by u 1.0. At (0.813, 0.159) — the very cell this devlog singles out as the one the new stops cannot reach — the old glare was 17 times the figure quoted. The stops were MIS-PLACED, not inert: a warm wash at the frame's right edge and almost nothing where the photo's own glare is. The conclusion (put the glare in photo u) survives; the framing did not.

**2. `mean: C.hillMid` was the wrong normalisation.** `albedoScaleOf` is exact only at the mean it is handed, and this texture's mean is not any of its stops — the glare reaches a fifth of its width and the rest sits near the deepest stop. `makeHillTexture` now computes the mean of its own drawn texels and the material takes that.

**3. Cell (0.146, 0.386) is not a colour problem.** The brief lists it as "house 1's timber, still light and now greenish". `npm run probe` says the first hit there is **`near ridge` at 1004 m** — a distant mountain card seen through a hole in the left row — and the "greenish" is `nearRidge` 0x6b7a5f itself. (0.104, 0.386) is the same hole. No colour on any timber can reach either.

**4. `ground base` was most of poses 2 and 6.** `out/scratch/posefind.mjs` (views.js's pose application plus probe.js's raycast; probe.js itself cannot answer, being pinned to the photo camera) put the pale mass filling pose 6's whole background at `ground base`, 82 to 163 m away — the flat box from y -30 to -24, fogged to nearly the fog colour. The "hard fog bands" are its own edges and `THREE.Fog`'s 150 m far plane across it.

## The sky

`bymesh` split the 69 sky cells into three faults, all visible in `out/skypair.png` (photo beside render, u 0 to 0.62, v 0 to 0.34):

- **The photo's blue top-left rendered lavender.** `cirrus` was 0xd8cfd2, a pink-grey read off cells (0.10, 0.05) and (0.15, 0.09) — cloud mixed with the sky behind it — and it was painted over the whole cirrus field; the cumulus puffs' falloff, `exp(-theta / 30.0)`, still carried 24% of their pink at theta = 43, which is the photo's own top row at u 0.23. Cells (0.229, 0.068), (0.271, 0.068) and (0.313, 0.068) read #c5d6e4, #c8d9e7 and #cddde9 in the photo against #d1c3cd, #d7c1c7 and #d3c3cf.
  **Fixed** by making every cloud colour a function of how much of the sun reaches that direction rather than of how thick the streak is, and by tightening the puffs to `exp(-theta / 18.0)`. `cirrus` is now a cloud's own body in the blue, box (0.30,0.05)-(0.37,0.075) **#cbdcea** — two levels off the clear sky beside it at (0.40,0.035)-(0.50,0.055) **#cfe0ed**, which is the measurement that says a cloud up there is not a different colour from the sky, only a different texture.
- **The warm band was pale pink where the photo is orange.** `skyWarmFar` 0xecd3bc against box (0.20,0.13)-(0.45,0.19) **#e7bfa2**; shipped at 0xe9c5ab, between the two, because the row at v 0.205 wants lighter than the box and the row at v 0.159 wants darker.
- **The glare near the sun was a pale cream wash.** `skyHalo` 0xf6e4cf → **0xf7dcb2**, spread from `exp(-theta / 8.0) * 0.8` to `exp(-theta / 10.0) * 0.9`, with `elevWeight` raised from 25 to 45 so the spread does not climb into the blue top row six degrees above the sun.

Sky summed cell distance **4.208 → 3.729 over the same 69 cells** measured on the sky work alone, and **3.607 on the shipped tree** — the rest came from the hill's own skyline moving, which changes what the cells along it average.

**Measured and rejected:** widening the warm band toward the sun (`4.5 + 6.0 * exp(-az / 18.0)`). It reaches the two cells it was written for and costs +0.025 each at (0.229, 0.023), (0.271, 0.023), (0.313, 0.023) and (0.354, 0.023), where the photo is clean blue. Also rejected: starting the deep-blue azimuth ramp at 18 degrees instead of 30 — the frame's own left edge is at about 40 degrees of azimuth, so the ramp is nearly all of it outside the photo, and the photo's top row is flat at u 0.06 to 0.15 (#99bedc, #9bc4e4, #9bc3e5, all within two levels of `skyTopBlue`). `skyTopGrey` is now 0x8fb6da, a deeper blue rather than a slate, and it is marked in `src/layout.js` as set by eye because the sky it paints is not in the photo.

**The silhouette is now trees.** The polyline is the top of the tallest trees; `makeHillTexture` takes it plus a 0.015-of-frame strip below it and cuts that strip's alpha into crowns and gaps from three scales of noise (single crowns about 3 px of the 1200 px frame, groups, whole stands), with a mean canopy of about 0.72 so the average skyline sits a third of the strip below the polyline and the tallest stands touch it. `alphaTest`, not `transparent`: the hill has to write depth like the hill it is. A mean of 0.6 was tried and opens the skyline too far — cell (0.729, 0.114) rendered #dccfb6 against a photo of #9f9d96.

**The shipped mean is not free at that cell either, and the first draft quoted only the rejected variant's cost.** At 0.72 the same cell renders **#d2c3ac** — still about 50 levels light — and it goes **0.1040 → 0.1521, +0.048, the largest single regression of the whole hill unit.** The cell straddles the tree line: the photo's mean there is half hill and half sky and the render's is more sky than hill. Lowering `TREE_RISE` or raising the canopy mean closes it and shuts the skyline, which is the thing the unit exists for.

**Bound of the tree line, in the tool's header:** the crowns are a function of photo u alone, so the skyline is a cut-out and has the same shape from every angle. At 300 to 500 m that is what a tree line looks like; it would flatten for a camera far enough to the side, and no pose in `npm run views` is near that.

**The stops were fitted through the measured transfer, and the fit did not land.** A texel on this hill does not display as its own value: the frame's bloom carries the blazing sky next to it and the tone curve lifts it. Two measured points, old texture value to rendered cell — (74,65,57) → (116,95,71) and (92,88,79) → (153,135,108) — give a per-channel affine map, and the stops were solved from it against the photo's own cells at (0.646, 0.159) #dcb698, (0.729, 0.159) #625e55 and (0.771, 0.159) #575653. Shipped: `hillRidge` 0x91816f, `hillHaze` 0x776d61, `hillMid` 0x5e5a54, `hillDeep` 0x3c3c40. `hillDeep` is new — `C.hill` was both the hill's deepest stop and the far ground's colour, so tuning one moved the other.

**Only one of the three solved cells improved**, and the critic is the reason that is written here rather than buried:

| cell | photo | base → final | distance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.646, 0.159 | #dcb698 | #c7af7b → #e1d3b3 | 0.0820 → **0.0902** |
| 0.729, 0.159 | #625e55 | #7b654b → #88745d | 0.0641 → **0.1027** |

Over all six cells the hill is first under, **0.474 → 0.499, worse by 0.025.** The hill unit's SSIM gain of +0.0058 is the tree line and the skyline's new position; the colours cost a little and did not pay for themselves.

**Why the fit is unreliable, so the next attempt does not repeat it.** The material now takes `mean: map.userData.mean`, computed from the drawn texels — which are a function of the stops being solved for. Changing a stop rescales the whole texture and moves the transfer the stop was solved against, so two measured points cannot fit a map whose slope depends on the answer. A next attempt should either iterate the fit to a fixed point or hold `mean` at a constant while solving.

One cell no single deepest stop can reach: (0.813, 0.159), photo #363124, where the photo's hill is in the right house's own shadow. The glare there is 0.006, so the render gives the deepest stop, about #575753, and making the deepest stop that dark takes the whole hill with it.

That is now a runnable probe rather than an assertion — `out/scratch/cardgap.mjs` re-evaluates `carryRight`, `ridgeVAt` and `canopyAt` from the source's own values with no browser, and on the shipped tree (24 points, because the cards end at u 1.55 now) it prints **zero firings on all three cards**. The parallax arithmetic itself is right and now applies to the real gap. ## The ground beyond the paving

`ground base` is gone and `outer ground` replaces it: one height-fielded, vertex-coloured surface, x +-320 and z +80 to -420, a shelf at about the street's level out to |x| = 13 falling away to the sides and, past the hillside's crest at z = -58, into the valley, with two scales of relief over it.

**What makes it safe is a ceiling, and the ceiling is exact.** In this camera model v depends only on a point's y and z and never on its x, so the height at which any (x, z) crosses a given photo row has a closed form: `y = 4.8 + z * 0.5989` is v = 0.78, and the surface is held 2.5 m below that for every z < 0. Below v 0.78 the photo frame is the near stairs, walls and paving from u 0 to 1, all a few metres away, so nothing here can win a depth test in the scored frame whatever the terrain does. The critic re-derived the constant (0.598884) and checked all 7865 of the surface's vertices: none is above the line.

**"It moved zero of 528 cells" is the arm it was first measured on, and not what shipped.** That arm (`scores-mtn2` → `scores-ground1`) moved one cell, by 0.0000006 of SSIM. But the ground was reworked four more times after it, and against the tree without any of it (`scores-hill8` 0.0640571 / 0.5407372 against the shipped 0.0640251 / 0.5403151) the whole ground unit **moves 12 cells, costs 0.00042 of SSIM and buys 0.00003 of cell distance**. `hill8` is the best SSIM any arm in this iteration reached. ## What the critic retracted, and what replaced it

One independent read-only critic, given the diff, the claim, every number, the file hashes and an instruction to say why the numbers do NOT support the claim. It verified before it attacked: `out/scores.json` = 0.06402511 / 0.54031506 against a base of 0.06600 / 0.52855, the thresholds at exactly the recorded margins, `test-final.log` complete and passing, 13 of 13 photo hexes quoted in new comments matching `cellsPhoto` exactly, the clearance figures against `out/clearance.json`, the ceiling constant re-derived to 0.598884 with the correct sign, and all 7865 `outer ground` vertices below the v = 0.78 line. Then it found the following. **Three of them are about how this lane measured rather than about the scene, and they are the ones to read.**

**1. A control measured on an arm that was reworked four times afterwards is a control for a different tree.** "`outer ground` moved zero of 528 cells" was `mtn2 → ground1`; the ground was then reworked four more times and what shipped moves 12 cells and costs 0.00042 of SSIM. **Retracted**, with the real figure and the four cells in the section below.

**2. A colour fit that fails at two of its three solved cells is not a fit.** The hill's four stops were solved from a measured transfer against three photo cells; only one improved. Over the six cells the hill is first under, the summed distance went **0.474 → 0.499, worse by 0.025**. The hill's SSIM gain (+0.0058) is the **tree line**, not the colours. **Retracted**: the stops are now marked in `src/layout.js` as fitted-and-not-landed, with the mechanism (the material's `mean` is computed from the texels the stops produce, so the transfer moves while you solve against it) and what a next attempt should do instead.

**3. A clamp that never fires means the arithmetic justified nothing.** `carryRight`'s `Math.max(own, ridgeVAt(u) + margin)` — the critic checked all 33 points of the tree it reviewed and `own` wins every one. A paragraph of parallax arithmetic was defending margins of 0.045 / 0.065 / 0.085 that no vertex depends on. **Retracted**: the operative clearance is `own` minus the hill's own opaque line, the margins are now ordered so the card needing most gets most (they were backwards), and the comment says the clamp is a guard that does not fire. The critic also computed the exposure threshold, which the margins had hidden: the cards come out from behind the hill at a reachable eye height, and `controls.maxDistance` is 120 m with no clamp on the polar angle from above. My own note that it was safe "within a factor of five" compared against the margins, which do not govern.

The numbers are now a probe rather than either party's assertion, and getting there took a third answer. `out/scratch/cardgap.mjs` re-evaluates `carryRight`, `ridgeVAt` AND `canopyAt` from the source with no browser. On the shipped tree: **zero firings on all 24 points**, a tightest gap of **0.1347 of frame** (`mountains farthest` at u 0.68) and exposure at **+89.3 m**, with `mountains blue` at 0.1531 and +113.5 m and `near ridge` at 0.1924 and +208.1 m — against a reachable rise of **115.95 m**, so **two of the three are reachable** and the second by 2.4 m.

**Both of the first two answers were wrong, in opposite directions, for the same reason.** I measured the gap to the ridge POLYLINE and got 0.1403; the critic measured it to the bottom of the whole tree strip and got 0.125. Neither is the hill's opaque line: the polyline is the top of the tallest trees and the strip below it is cut into crowns and gaps, so a card edge inside the strip shows through only as far as `canopyAt` at that column allows. The opaque line is `ridgeVAt + TREE_RISE * (1 - canopyAt)` — at u 0.68, v 0.13959 against a polyline of 0.13400. Two more corrections came with it: the occluding edge is at the depth of that row, **481 m and not the ridge's 500**, and `controls.maxDistance` = 120 is measured from the ORBIT TARGET, which sits at y 0.751, so the reachable rise is 115.95 m and not 120. The feature this iteration added — the tree line — is exactly what made the old way of measuring wrong.

## Traps for the next session

- **Ask `out/scratch/bymesh.mjs` first.** It put the SKY at the top with 4.208 of summed cell distance over 69 cells, and the sky was not on the brief's list. Iteration 3 learned the same thing about the right walkway. The twenty worst cells are not the same question as which mesh owns the error.
- **A cell that will not move for three iterations may not be a colour at all.** (0.146, 0.386) was on the list from iteration 1 as house 1's timber. `npm run probe` says the first hit is a mountain card 1004 m away. One probe would have closed it at any point in those three iterations.
- **A texture's parameters are in the texture's coordinates, not the photo's.** `makeHillTexture` took `sunU = 0.62` meaning a photo column and evaluated it against `x / size`, and the hill's whole colour ramp had been inert since it was written. The tell was that resampling those stops never moved anything.
- **`albedoScaleOf` is exact only at the mean it is handed.** The hill's texture was normalised against `C.hillMid`, one of four stops and not the texture's own average. `makeHillTexture` computes and returns its own mean now.
- **A texel does not display as its own value.** Between the bloom carrying the blazing sky beside the hill and the tone curve, the measured transfer on this surface is about `2.06 * T - 36` on red. Two measured points give an affine fit per channel and the stops can then be solved for the cells; guessing costs a render each time.
- **v depends only on y and z in this camera, never on x.** `y = 4.8 + z * 0.5989` is exactly v = 0.78 for any x, and holding a surface below it is a proof that it cannot enter the scored frame above that row. `outer ground` is 320 m wide and 500 m deep and moved zero of 528 cells.
- **A crown grid in texels ties the grain to the texture's extent.** Carrying the hill out to u -3.0 made every crown three and a half times wider in the frame with the same `CW = 760`. The density belongs in photo units.
- **`npm run clearance` catches terrain.** The first `outer ground` sat 0.4 m proud of the paving across the whole road at z = -0.25 and the gate named it in one line. Nothing else in the suite would have.
- **The far street's paving was 7.5 m longer than the street.** Before adding anything at the bend, check where `L.FAR_STREET_END` is against what stands there.
- **A `npm test` piped to `tail` reports tail's exit status.** The mid-session run in this session did that and its own log had already scrolled the first six gates away; the shipped run is `npm test > out/scratch/test-final.log 2>&1` with the status read from `$?`.
- **A patch script that checks "did the file change at all" reports a half-applied patch as a pass.** This session lost a `body: false` guard that way: the first of two replacements landed, the whole-string comparison at the end was therefore satisfied, and a data flag sat in `farHouses` for hours with no code reading it. Every scratch patch here now throws on the first anchor it cannot find, before writing anything. What actually caught it was the draw-call and triangle counts not moving when the flag was removed — read the artifact, not the tool's word.
- **A control belongs to the arm it was measured on.** "`outer ground` moved zero of 528 cells" was true of `mtn2 → ground1` and the ground was reworked four times after it; what shipped costs 0.0004 of SSIM. Re-measure a control against the tree that ships, or say which arm it belongs to.
- **A clamp that never fires justifies nothing.** `carryRight`'s three margins carried a paragraph of parallax arithmetic and not one shipped vertex depends on them, because the card's own line always wins the `Math.max`. Before defending a constant, check whether the branch it lives on is ever taken — the critic did it by evaluating every point, which took it one file and no browser.
- **`renderer.info.render` is a per-frame statistic, not a scene census.** The shot's draw calls moved 364 → 366 across a batch that adds no mesh, because frustum culling changed. `out/scratch/census.mjs` walks the graph and prints every mesh with its instances and triangles, which is the thing to diff when a total moves.
- **A gradient card's interior depends on its triangulation.** Shortening the range cards by three outline points each changed how `ShapeGeometry` triangulates them, and the per-vertex colours then interpolate over different triangles — `out/render.png` moved for a change whose geometry is entirely off-frame.
- **An occlusion argument is not an off-frame argument.** `leftRowBacks` was defended as outside the photo frame on the strength of one corner; two of its four corners are inside it and the geometry is merely hidden. Project every corner, or run the control.
