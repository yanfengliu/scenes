## What the checks measure, and what they refuse to read

Both measure the BUILT SCENE. `tools/clearance.js` imports nothing from `src/`, and that is the one design decision worth defending: both defects were introduced by editing exactly the constants a source-derived check would have read (`HEADROOM`, `farRowFrontX`, `FAR_ROW.keepOff`), so a check written from them would have agreed with the defect. The paving surface comes from rays dropped onto the paving meshes; the canopy's height from the instance matrices and vertex positions three actually draws; the paved band's edges and centre from where the paving stops at each z; the swept window from the paving's own bounding box.

The headroom half samples 66,284 points — every instance of every `cherry *` mesh but the trunk, and every vertex of the merged strand mesh — of which 60,265 sit over the walkable street. Each is measured against the highest walkable paving under its own footprint (its centre and its four horizontal corners, on a 0.25 m ground cache). The street half sweeps 200 paved z positions every 0.25 m, x every 0.05 m, and asserts the 166 of them above z −33.5.

## The limits are written from the person, not from the scene

1.90 m is a standing eye — 1.70 m over this paving, the eye `npm run views` pose 5 puts there and the eye the pose-5 blossom was seen from — plus 0.20 m. It is deliberately not 2.4 m, the scene's own `HEADROOM`: a gate set to the constant it guards permits nothing and proves nothing. 0.60 m is under half a doorway, and 0.55 m is what a person keeps once the far row's eave has taken its 0.40 m of oversail.

**The 0.173 m of margin over the shipped 2.073 m is not comfortable, and calling it an envelope for design change would be wrong.** The canopy draws every card's size and rotation from one shared PRNG stream, so any edit that changes how many `rand()` calls precede it re-rolls all 31,368 cards: six mutations aimed at the limbs and sub-branches, none of which touched `HEADROOM`, the mask, `streetY` or `streetCenterX`, moved this reading across 2.045 to 2.206 m — a 0.161 m spread, comparable to the margin. The figure reproduces exactly between runs and does not hold still across edits. When an unrelated canopy edit reds this gate the answer is to look at the frame, not to lower the limit. An independent critic found this by reading the six mutation logs against each other; the first draft of this paragraph confused reproducibility across runs with stability across edits.

The other thing the margin is spent on is card size, and the number for that is the vertical drop from a card's centre to its lowest corner — **0.310 m**, measured and printed every run. An earlier draft quoted 0.617 m, which is the widest card's HORIZONTAL extent and sets nothing.

Where each limit actually bites was measured, not extrapolated, because two points are not a slope. `HEADROOM` reds at 2.25 and at 2.20 (both read 1.799 m) and passes at 2.30 and 2.35. `FAR_ROW.keepOff` passes at 1.60, where the eave rule reads exactly 0.55 m, and reds at 1.75. An earlier draft of this file said the keepOff boundary was "about 1.9 m", which was wrong in the permissive direction and is the kind of line a next session relies on.

## Three design errors found before they shipped

**The blocker test read the column, not the structure.** The first version asked whether the topmost structure over the patch was higher than a kerb and whether anything was below head height, and ANDed them. On this scene it gave the right answer everywhere. It is still wrong: a 0.17 m coping with a roof eight metres above it satisfies both, so any coping under any roof would have read as a building in the road.

**Then the corrected surface test found a hole under itself.** Asking "is there a structure surface between 0.35 m and 2.0 m over the paving" cannot confuse a coping with a wall — but every hit a downward ray returns is a TOP face, because the materials are front-facing, so a wall running from below the paving to above head height presents no surface in the window at all and is invisible. The far row is caught today only because its base band's top happens to sit 0.45 m up. So the sweep now forces every material double-sided for its duration and restores it after (nothing is rendered while it is on), which returns each solid's entry and exit, and reads the air per INSTANCE rather than per mesh so a tile set's high and low pieces do not merge into one span. The test then asks what it actually means to ask: **does a structure occupy the air over this paving, between the height a kerb stops and the height a person does.**

**And that stronger test then went red on shipped code**, at z −33.75 to −42, blocked by `corner house body`, `corner house roof` and `bend roof`. Not a false positive: `far street slabs` run to z = −42, eleven metres past the bend, underneath the block-out that closes the street, and over 27 of those 34 z positions the near half has under 0.60 m of road. It is also not this gate's defect to report — iteration 2 already named it, the far houses standing where the photo has the far street's own paving.

## Where the street ends, and why that is a literal rather than a rule

The first answer was a rule: the street runs to the first z built over from edge to edge, and nothing past that is asserted. It passed, and the critic showed why it was worse than the carve-out it was avoiding. It is **monotone the wrong way** — a building that intrudes partly fails, a building that blocks the road completely is reclassified as "not road" and passes — and the switch was thrown by a single 0.25 m row of block-out, which silenced the 8.25 m behind it including the 26 rows where `corner house body` stands in the road at head height.

So the bound is now a literal: `STREET_TO_Z = -33.5`. Be clear about where that number came from — **it was chosen after seeing where the gate went red, and on this tree it exempts exactly the same 34 rows the rule exempted.** Not one row changed status. What makes it better is not the answer, which is identical, but that it cannot be widened by building more. Everything above it is asserted, **including any z that a building closes from edge to edge**, because a full closure gives a near-half run of 0 and fails like any other intrusion.

And because a carve-out nobody measures can grow, the exempt region is pinned from both sides: no more than 40 paved rows past the bound (34 today) and no blockers in it but the three that are there (`corner house body`, `corner house roof`, `bend roof`). Running the machiya row past the bend with `FAR_ROW.zFar` −30.5 → −36.0 is a recorded red proof of that pin. Seven of the 34 exempt rows are in fact open road with no blocker at all, which the printed line says.

## What the checks refuse to cover, and why each refusal is a measurement

- **`petals` are excluded**, and this is the one place where the check is narrower than the task that asked for it. Two reasons, both measured. A petal's drawn height is `instanceY − mod(uTime * speed, 7.5)` computed in the vertex shader, so the instance matrix a CPU-side check can read is not where the petal is drawn. And petals are built to fall through the street and below it — **182 of the 420 sit over the walkable street at t = 0, 68 of those below 1.7 m**, and the lowest is 1.86 m *under* the paving. A matrix-read check on petals would be measuring the wrong number and would fail a correct scene. If the drift is ever to be bounded, it has to be bounded in the shader where it is computed.
- **The limbs are covered only by their spread.** They live in the `cherry trunk` mesh, which also carries the trunk, and the trunk stands on the street by design. What is asserted, over the street and over the terrace separately, is that the mesh's low wood is all one clump around its own deepest point — 0.87 m and 0.86 m of spread today, against a 2.00 m limit. Low wood 1.9 m from the foot still passes. The spread is measured from the deepest point and not from the centroid of the low points, because a centroid is dragged toward an intruding limb and the limit then permits about 2.7 m rather than the 2.00 it names; an independent critic worked that out from the arithmetic.
- **A clear RUN in each half is not a clear half.** Bands in the asserted range are 4.05 m to 7.00 m wide, so a structure may occupy several metres of road at any z with every assertion green. The rule is "you can walk down each side", not "the road is empty". A structure on ground that is not paving is invisible too, and so is a zero-thickness vertical sheet — a downward ray runs parallel to it and returns nothing. The scene has one such mesh, `noren`, and it stands off the paved band.
- **The canopy's height comes from CPU matrices**, which is where the cards are drawn in y only because the wind offset has no y term (`vec3(s, 0.0, c * 0.4)` in `src/animation.js`). Add one and this gate goes on reporting the still positions. This is the bound most likely to be invalidated by a change that looks unrelated.
- **A new hanging plant under a name that does not start with `cherry `** is measured by nothing. Every plant the gate does not measure is printed by name every run, which is a weak defence: it prints the list a reader skims.

## `npm run views`: the stall was not the contact sheet

The report was that `views` writes all seven poses and then hangs for over twenty minutes with no output and no error. The obvious suspect was the contact sheet — seven 1.4 MB PNGs inlined as base64 data URLs into one `page.evaluate`, about 13 MB of argument — and the obvious fix was to drop it, which the canon supports anyway.

That would not have fixed it. Timed against the same browser, on the scene page (`out/scratch/views-stall.log`):

| | scene page |
| --- | --- |
| no-op `page.evaluate`, tiny argument | **4.96 s** |
| no-op `page.evaluate`, one 1.4 MB data URL | **4.74 s** |
| `decodeImage` of one pose down to 120 px | **80.71 s** |
| `decodeImage` of all seven — the black-frame check | **792.12 s** |

The argument size is not the cause: carrying 1.4 MB costs less than carrying nothing. The cause is the scene page itself, which runs a requestAnimationFrame loop rendering 1200x1100 through the post chain; under SwiftShader that is about five seconds a frame, and it both queues every `await` inside an evaluate behind a whole frame and saturates the CPU the decode needs. **The split between those two is not measured**, and the arithmetic does not close on queueing alone: `decodeImage`'s page function has three awaits, so about four hops, and 80.71 / 4.96 is sixteen. Both mechanisms have the same remedy, which is why the fix works without the split being known; the probe rerun with the render loop stopped would separate them. Thirteen minutes of the stall were the *black-frame check*, which had to stay, and it ran before the sheet was ever reached, which is why the reproduction never printed a digest line.

So the fix is two things, and only the second one is the fix:

1. **The contact sheet is gone.** An aggregate view answers "is there one of each" and never "is each one right", both recorded sweep reviews were done one pose at a time at 1200x1100 with per-pose digests, and the tool's own header already said to work that way. `out/views/index.txt` replaces it: pose, sha256, mean luminance, eye, absolute path. Plain text on purpose — it cannot be looked at, so it cannot become the thing reviewed instead of the frames. The tool still deletes any `index.png` an older build left behind, which was checked by planting one. (`docs/devlog/detailed/2026-09-10-iteration-1.md` describes the sheet as current in two places; that is history and stays, but it is superseded here.)
2. **Every image operation after a pose is rendered runs on a second, blank page** in the same browser, which has no render loop to queue behind or compete with.

Measured after, on the shipped bytes: **25 s of page load, 323 s of poses and 6.8 s of inspection** — five and a half minutes in all, against 792 s for the inspection alone before — and every one of the seven pose digests is identical to the run before the change — `1-photo` d29b76dd0272, `2-orbit-left-up` 29ca31432978, `3-orbit-right-up` dacd2580a5e8, `4-paving-down-steps` 33dcbda34163, `5-landing-look-back` 6f3c19e03eb4, `6-high-over-roofs` 7b5c75618ae4, `7-clamped-out-of-the-wall` c2c4d22e66ee. Those are also iteration 2's own recorded digests, so the change moved no pixels and the tree it ran on is the shipped one. The manifest's sweep digest, one value over all seven and the line a review should quote, is `beb2eef266b5`.

Progress is printed at every stage now — `[n/7]` and elapsed seconds per pose, then per frame in the inspection pass — because a tool that is slow and a tool that has hung look identical without it, which is the half of the report that was not about time at all.

## Costs

The whole suite, green on the tree this describes (`out/scratch/npm-test-final.log`, EXIT=0, scores 0.0749 / 0.4733):

    shot 116 s, compare 2 s, placement 59 s, clearance 34 s, animation 1420 s, nudge 274 s, blackframe 36 s, record 29 s

`npm run clearance` adds **34 s** of that, measured in the suite rather than standalone: about 13-15 s of page load and 5-6 s of work in the page (0.2 s canopy, 5.7 s sweep). The sweep is raycasting in JS and never touches the GPU, so it costs the same under SwiftShader as on a GPU; only the page load is renderer-bound. Filtering the ray sets is what makes it cheap — one ray against the walkable paving costs 0.11 ms and against the structures 0.15 ms, while one against the whole scene costs **4.49 ms**, because the canopy's 31,368 instances dominate every ray that passes near it.

## Traps for the next session

- **Never run an image operation on the scene page.** A no-op evaluate there costs a whole SwiftShader frame. `decodeImage`, `createImageBitmap`, `toDataURL` and anything else with an `await` in it belongs on a blank page.
- **`tools/clearance.js` imports nothing from `src/`.** That is deliberate and it is the property that makes it a gate rather than a restatement. Adding an import of `src/layout.js` to "simplify" it would quietly retire it. What it does copy is about 46 MESH NAMES, in four regexes, and **a partial rename fails silently**. Renaming `landing` and `landing slabs` together drops every canopy sample over the landing and leaves the gate green on a higher "lowest"; the canopy floors do NOT catch it, because they count geometry rather than ground and the stairs and far street still supply tens of thousands of samples. Renaming `cherry trunk` to `trunk` drops it out of both `CANOPY` and `LIVING`, and it is the trunk-mesh floor — added after round 2 of review — that catches that one. The floors are the part of this gate that has never been made to go red, and their calibration is argued from the shipped figures and nothing else.
- **Every limit is the gate's own claim, not the scene's constant**, and where each one bites was MEASURED rather than extrapolated. `HEADROOM` reds at 2.25 and 2.20 and passes at 2.30; `FAR_ROW.keepOff` passes at 1.60 and reds at 1.75; `FAR_ROW.depth` at 5.2 reds the far half; the two together at 1.70 and 3.4 red the eave rule, which is a combination an independent critic constructed to slip past the other two and is why the eave limit is 0.55 and not 0.40. There is a recorded red proof for each.
- **Past z −33.5 nothing is asserted**, because the bend's block-out stands on the paving there and leaves under 0.60 m of near half over 27 of 34 z positions. That number was chosen after seeing where the gate went red and it exempts the same rows an earlier rule did; what keeps it honest is the pin — no more than 40 rows and no blockers but the three named. Fix the far houses and the bend block-out and `STREET_TO_Z` can move down, and the gate gets stronger for free — the printed "past z -33.5" line is where to look.
- **Petals are the uncovered case.** They are the only canopy geometry that legitimately reaches head height over the street, they cannot be measured from their matrices, and 68 of the 182 over the street sit below a standing eye at t = 0. Anything that changes the drift changes that and nothing will say so.
- **There are now three hand-rolled `isMainModule` implementations under `tools/`** — `serve.js`'s (which `clearance.js` copies, as `docs/policies/local-rules.md` requires) and `views.js`'s `pathToFileURL` form, in the repo whose whole marker gate exists because one of them was wrong. Unifying them means editing the local rule that pins serve.js's form by description, which was outside this session's allowed changes. It is worth doing.
- **`src/vegetation.js:188` says the pose-5 card was "0.36 m away"** where the iteration 2 devlog's own table, and everything written since, says 0.26 m. `src/` was frozen this session so it was not corrected; the 0.26 m figure is the one the table supports.
- **`docs/devlog/summary.md` owes two lines**, one for iteration 2 and one for this session; both were outside this session's allowed changes. `docs/learning/lessons.md` reads "No open lessons", so iteration 2's "neither fix is gated" never entered the queue it was meant to — the gate-proofs entries above retire a lesson that was never filed, which is the same shape as the `compare` entry lower in that file.
