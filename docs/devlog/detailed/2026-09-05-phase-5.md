# 2026-09-05 — Phase 5: life, interaction, performance, delivery

## What was built

- `src/animation.js`: the wind, the petals, the drift and the sway, and the clock the gates pin. Everything that moves in quantity moves in the vertex shader: `applyWind` patches a material's `project_vertex` with an offset computed from the vertex's own world position and one time uniform, so the 30,000 blossom cards and the 758 strand tubes sway together for no per-frame CPU at all. The 420 petals are a second instanced mesh whose whole trajectory is a closed form of the instance's seed and the time, so they are one draw call and no update. Only the two lanterns move on the CPU, each hanging from a pivot at its bracket. The clouds drift by an offset into the sky shader's own noise coordinates.
- `tools/animation.js` (in `npm test`, also `npm run animation`): scores the photo view at seven points across the wind's slowest period and asserts the worst of them, so a drift that only shows at t = 3 s cannot hide behind a gate that shoots one frame. It prints the spread.
- The grounding half of `tools/placement.js`, retiring the phase 3 lesson: each listed object's own base is measured every run and a ray dropped from it, failing when the mesh it should stand on is too far below (floating) or too far above (buried). Proof in `docs/learning/gate-proofs.md`.
- Controls: a floor clamp from the street's own height field, a corridor clamp between the two house rows below eave height, touch bindings (one finger orbits, two pinch and pan), a device-pixel-ratio cap of 2 that survives a move to another screen, and a loading indicator that turns into the error message if the scene never becomes ready.
- Delivery: `README.md`, `docs/render.webp` (229 KiB of the 256 allowed, encoded by chromium itself in `tools/render-webp.js`, so no dependency was added), a Pages workflow that publishes the static files, and a CI workflow that runs `npm test` on a runner with no GPU.

## What was believed and proved false

- **A material patch can chain onto whatever was there before.** `applyWind` wraps `onBeforeCompile` and the cache key, and the first version called the previous key as a bare function. three's default `customProgramCacheKey` reads `this.onBeforeCompile`, so calling it unbound threw on the first frame that compiled a wind material, and the whole page failed to load. It is bound to the material now.
- **The scored shot is deterministic because the seeds are.** It was, until the scene started moving: the shot would have caught the canopy at whatever phase the load happened to reach. `npm run shot` now pins the clock at t = 0 before it screenshots, and `probe` and `placement` do the same, so every gate looks at the same frame.
- **A grounding check can test a point.** The first version stored a base coordinate in `src/layout.js` and asked whether the ground was under it. It stayed green when the object floated away, because the ground under the recorded point had not moved. It measures the object's own bounding box now.
- **The photo's sky blue is the colour sampled for it.** `skyTopBlue` was `#bdd5e8`, sampled from a cell that already carried some of the haze; the photo's own top row is `#9bc4e4`. Replacing it was worth 0.0004 of cell distance for no SSIM cost. Making the blue arrive sooner across the gradient, which looked like the same fix, cost 0.0012 of SSIM and was dropped.
- **The landing is one colour.** Its far end runs into the shade of the bend: the photo reads `#323840` at v 0.75 against `#4a4950` at v 0.70, while the render had it uniformly light. A shade that falls to 0.68 over the last 7 m was worth 0.0004 of cell distance and 0.005 of SSIM, the largest single gain left.

## The animation's cost, measured

Seven frames across the wind's slowest period (2π/0.72, about 8.7 s):

| t (s) | Cell distance | SSIM |
| ----- | ------------- | ---- |
| 0.0 | 0.0820 | 0.4339 |
| 1.4 | 0.0820 | 0.4338 |
| 2.9 | 0.0820 | 0.4339 |
| 4.3 | 0.0820 | 0.4342 |
| 5.8 | 0.0821 | 0.4336 |
| 7.3 | 0.0821 | 0.4338 |
| 8.7 | 0.0820 | 0.4340 |

The spread is 0.0001 of cell distance and 0.0005 of SSIM. Those numbers are from the run before the sky and landing changes; the gate re-runs them on every `npm test`, and `out/animation/scores.json` holds the latest.

The amplitudes that produce it: the canopy sways 8.5 cm at the bottom of a strand, growing as the square of the depth below the crown so the top of the tree is still; the petals fall at 0.45 to 0.95 m/s through a 7.5 m band and wrap; the clouds drift 0.004 radians per second; the lanterns swing 0.035 radians.

**The scores do not constrain those amplitudes, and the first version of this gate pretended otherwise.** The critic raised the canopy's sway fourteen-fold, to 1.2 m, and the gate passed it: cell distance and SSIM barely move when the canopy sways, because its mean color is much the same wherever it is. The gate now also measures the mean per-pixel change between neighbouring frames, which does rise with the amplitude (0.18 levels at the shipped sway, 0.65 at fourteen times it) and is bounded from both sides, so an animation that becomes violent fails and one that quietly stops moving fails too. The proof of the red run is in `docs/learning/gate-proofs.md`. What keeps the motion subtle is that bound, not the scores.

## What the critic found, and what changed

The critic reproduced every measurement, drove the live page to check the clamps (seven adversarial drags bottomed out at exactly the 0.45 m clearance), confirmed the reset button and `R`, the pixel-ratio cap at a device ratio of 3, the touch bindings, the resize handling, the webp's format and size, and that the petals, lanterns and clouds really move. Its findings:

1. **The animation gate did not constrain the animation.** A fourteen-fold canopy sway passed it, and the devlog claimed the amplitudes were "what the scores allow". Both fixed: the gate bounds the spread far more tightly and adds the motion measure that actually discriminates, and it has now been seen red. The devlog says what the gate proves.
2. **The licence contradicted the README about the photograph.** `LICENSE` was MIT over everything; the README said the photograph was not covered. The licence now carves `japan.webp` out explicitly and the README points at it.
3. **CI would probably hit its own timeout.** The animation gate is minutes per frame on a software renderer. The timeout is 60 minutes and CI runs three frames through `ANIMATION_FRAMES`.
4. **`out/animation/` kept frames from older runs beside the current ones**, so anyone reading a failure could read the wrong frame. The directory is cleared first.
5. **The wind is not in the shadow pass**, because three renders shadows with its own depth material: the strands and the petals were casting still shadows while their display geometry swayed. Both are excluded from casting now, as the blossom cards already were.
6. **`timeUniforms` could grow on every recompile.** It de-duplicates.
7. **Docs**: the README described only half the placement gate and omitted `architecture.js`; AGENTS.md's module list was missing `animation.js`. All corrected.

Two things it raised are carried, not fixed. The plan's "no console warnings" is true on the software renderer the gates use but not on the GPU path, where three's own program logs one shader precision warning; nothing gates warnings, only errors. And the corridor clamp lets the camera 0.35 m past the wall faces, which is deliberate looseness so the useful low views survive, and recoverable with `R`.

## The user's flicker, and what it turned out to be

The user said "As I move the camera around it flickers a lot". The measurements are in `docs/learning/defect-register.md`; what is worth keeping here is how the diagnosis went, because the first hypothesis was wrong and the first fix was worse than wrong.

The hypothesis handed over was depth fighting between the surfaces phase 2 stacked centimetres apart, which fitted the symptom and the worst blocks (house 1's roof and dormer, the fence roof). It was false, and three measurements killed it: the composer's targets carry 24-bit depth, not 16; moving the camera's near from 0.2 to 1.0 or 2.0 and far from 6000 to 400 changed the photo view's figure not at all; and an amplified difference image put every changed pixel on an edge, with flat interiors untouched, which is the opposite of what a depth fight looks like. The critic then pushed depth precision a thousand times worse (near 0.002) and the figure still did not move, and classified the drastically changed pixels by their surroundings: only 0.3% of them sit in flat neighbourhoods, though flat regions are half the frame.

What it is: geometry thinner than a pixel. The cherry's strand tubes are about 0.35 px wide at their distance; so are tile ridges, lattice slats and the edges of thirty thousand blossom cards. A triangle that lands between the samples covers one or none.

The first fix supersampled at 1.5x and halved the drastic changes, and the critic found two things wrong with it. It sized the render target from `renderer.getSize()`, which is CSS pixels, so at a device pixel ratio of 2 the target was a quarter of the canvas and the scene was rendered small and scaled up. And its own cap, `min(1.5, 2 / pixelRatio)`, was exactly 1 at ratio 2, on the reasoning that a dense display is already sampling finely. Measurement says otherwise: at ratio 2 the unfixed scene is *less* stable by this metric, not more, so the fix switched itself off exactly where it was most needed. Every retina display, every phone, and Windows at 200% got nothing.

It also found a supporting fact false. Asking for eight multisamples had produced byte-identical output, which I wrote down as SwiftShader ignoring the request; in fact SwiftShader's maximum is four and WebGL clamps, so the request worked and could not go higher there, while a desktop GPU reports eight and uses them. Multisampling is carrying more than half of the stability, and it was nearly abandoned on that false premise.

The fix now: eight requested samples, the target sized from the drawing buffer, and a supersample of 1.2 rather than 1.5, because 1.2, 1.35, 1.5 and 2.0 all measure the same and 1.2 costs 1.44x the fragments instead of 2.25x. Median frame time went 2.6 ms to 3.2 ms, not to 6.3 ms.

## Bounds worth knowing

- The animation gate costs about six minutes under SwiftShader, which is most of `npm test`'s runtime. Seven frames is the compromise between covering the cycle and keeping the gate usable, and `ANIMATION_FRAMES` trims it where a run has to be quick; a longer cycle (the petals wrap on a period of tens of seconds, not 8.7) is not fully covered.
- The motion bound measures the whole frame, so a violent animation confined to a small part of the scene could still pass under it.
- The nudge gate has about one bit of resolution on the supersample: every scale from 1.2 to 2.0 passes alike and only turning it off fails. It proves the class of defect, not the setting.
- The scene is still least stable close to the paving at a device pixel ratio of 2 (0.50% of pixels change drastically under a 2 mm nudge, against 0.28% at ratio 1). Those pixels are a quarter of the screen area each, so the same physical shimmer counts more of them; the gate's limits are per ratio for that reason.
- The scored shot now renders at 1.2x and resolves down, which is the same thing the page does, so the metric is not being shown something the user is not.
- The camera clamps are a floor from the street's height field and a corridor between the two house rows below eave height. They do not model the buildings: the camera can still be pushed inside the far houses or the hill, which are far from anywhere a viewer goes. The limits were kept loose on purpose, so the views the manager found useful still work.
- The petals fall in a box around the canopy rather than on a true wind field, and they do not interact with anything.
- CI runs the gates on SwiftShader with no GPU, which is what the scored shot has always used, so the numbers should match. `npm run perf` is deliberately not run there: it measures the machine, and a shared runner's number would fail forever while telling nobody anything.
- `docs/render.webp` is 229 KiB at quality 0.92. The encoder walks the quality down until it fits, so a future render that compresses worse will come out softer rather than over the limit.
