## Traps for the next session

- **A flat plane at the right mean is not a solved surface.** `right walkway` had the photo's own mean to five levels and the worst summed error in the frame, because the cell metric scores variance and a flat plane has none. Ask `out/scratch/bymesh.mjs` which mesh owns the most error BEFORE choosing colours; the twenty worst cells did not name this one.
- **A slab's own edge is a sixth of what a cell sees at 35 degrees, and it is unlit.** Any paved surface added to the right side needs thin slabs or its colours will be read back 25 to 50 percent dark with the stone itself measured on target.
- **Fit a ramp to the slab ROWS, not to the cell grid.** Rows here project to 0.056 of frame and cells are 0.045, so one row spans two cells and the two cells can want opposite things. Three positions of the same shadow edge scored 1.588, 1.347 and 1.835 over the same twenty cells.
- **`lattice()` can be split vertically for free and not along z.** It draws twice per slat and the slat count comes from the z range alone.
- **`tileRoof` now takes an optional `colorOf(pointOnPlane)`.** It draws nothing and a roof that omits it lays the same tiles from the same stream, which is how the annex roof's far end was darkened without renumbering any tile on any roof.
- **A card cluster has to be solved against the ray, not nudged toward it.** The `fence spill` placement check failed three times on guesses at 0.1 m each: the cluster was behind the boards, then half behind them, then 0.06 m too low, because the ray CLIMBS as it goes out (y 2.42 at x 2.01, y 2.09 at x 2.29). Substituting the ray into the ellipsoid took one minute and settled it, and it also showed that the obvious next nudge would have started occluding the `shrub` check one cell away.
- **`shrubLit` is a photo cell reading and `shrub` is a bush's shaded interior.** A cluster meant to BE that cell takes `shrubLit` at both ends; taking `shrub` renders grey leaves where the photo is green.
- **A painted shadow has a ceiling, and it is the rig's own.** With `RIG` at ambient 0.78, sun 0.18, sky 0.14, ground 0.10 and fill 0.06, the deepest CAST shadow this scene can throw removes 14% of the light. Anything painted much darker than that sits beside real shadows and reads as paint, however well it scores. The walkway's near end was painted at 81% for one round and pose 4 stepped 80 levels of luma across a single slab joint. Work the ratio out before choosing the hex.
- **A colour on a surface the photo frame does not reach is answerable only to the sweep.** The photo's bottom edge stops at z = -4.665 on the right terrace, so every slab row in front of that is free: narrowing the near band from 2.48 m to 1.24 m moved zero of 528 cells. The converse is the trap — a term written for two scored cells can fire entirely outside the frame and cost nothing but ugliness, which is what the walkway's corner term did.
- **`git grep "export function foo"` is not a check that `foo` is exported.** `tools/lib/browser.js` exports `openInspector` with `export async function`, my pattern did not allow the `async`, and I reported a defect on main on the strength of a pattern that could not match. The worktree's own copy really was missing the export -- it is based on the commit before 1e3f03b restored it -- so the symptom was real and the diagnosis was not. `git merge main` fixed it and moved no pixels.
- **Anything built on the far row's street face spends the clearance margin directly**, because the gate measures the row's wall line for its 0.60 m rule. 2 cm proud moved the reported minimum from 1.10 m at z -22.25 to 0.95 m at z -29.25 — not because 0.15 m was spent, but because the minimum moved to a z where the base band's own run was already 0.97 m. Measure both ways before quoting a cost.
