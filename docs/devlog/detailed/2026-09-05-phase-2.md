# 2026-09-05 — Phase 2: architecture and stone

## What was built

- `src/textures.js`: per-pixel canvas albedos (stone, rubble, wood boards, plaster, kawara, noren cloth, sudare, sign, lantern paper, glaze) with a two-pass mean correction so every texture averages exactly to the material's photo-sampled sRGB mean; height fields give tangent-space normal maps and roughness maps, stored in `material.userData.pbr` for phase 4's lit materials. Materials stay unlit (`MeshBasicMaterial`) because the photo colors are already lit.
- `src/instancing.js`: `instanced()` builds an InstancedMesh from transforms with a per-instance tint around 1 and a per-instance UV offset (a `MeshBasicMaterial` patched through `onBeforeCompile`, so shared geometry does not repeat the same texel pattern); geometries for bevelled slabs, rounded and square wall stones, S-section pan tiles, half-round ridge tiles and round eave caps.
- `src/paving.js`: treads and risers on the 30 steps (four slabs across, staggered, 2 cm joints over a darker mortar body 3 cm below and 3 cm behind the risers), the stepped gutter with a curb and its own stepped mortar, slab grids on the landing and the street following the height field and the bend, an eight-step side stair on a rubble body, and the raised top landing with a coping row over a stone-block front wall.
- `src/walls.js`: `stackedStones()` lays courses along any segment between two height lines (ashlar or rubble) with the blocks' faces on the line and the mortar body 5 cm behind; the near ashlar wall with its lighter top course, the rubble retaining walls on both sides, the plaster low wall with a low gabled tile cap, the fence's stone-faced core that jogs back around the head of the side stair and runs down into the wall's notch, and lathe pots.
- `src/roofs.js`: `tileRoof()` tiles any four-corner roof plane: rows parallel to the eave, tiles clipped to the parallelogram and to an optional plan test (`keep`), eave caps, ridge tiles on gables, a board below, a fascia and rafter ends; `collect` gathers several planes into shared instanced meshes. Nine roof planes on the houses, a dormer on house 1, and the fence's small gabled roof (ten planes meeting at hips and valleys, three meshes).
- `src/facades.js`: textured board and plaster walls, lattices and shoji grids as instanced slats, a lattice window, the balcony (floor, posts, rails), sliding doors standing in front of their wall, the white awning as a sheet over the canopy's lower rows, the sign on a bracket at house 3's far corner, two ribbed paper lanterns on brackets by the entrance, the fence boards with dark posts, the lamp post with a four-panel head, and the noren as a 60x10 cloth mesh with three panels sagging between hangers, a bow toward the street and a scalloped hem.

## What was believed and proved false

- **The ground-floor eave of the right machiya is level.** With level tiles the photo's blue band (v 0.33 to 0.40 from u 1.0 to about 0.75) lost its lower half to fascia and soffit at u 0.7 to 0.8. Its outer edge stays at v 0.40 across most of its length, which for an edge below the eye means it descends with distance: the eave steps down 0.5 m along the street (`RIGHT_MACHIYA.eaveDrop`), and the ground floor's top follows.
- **A cross product gives the roof's upward normal.** `cross(along, upslope)` points down when the slope runs toward +x, which put the tiles of the right eave, the right top roof and house 1's back slope under their own boards. The tiler now walks the eave the other way when that happens, keeping the basis right-handed.
- **House 1's top roof eave is at 6.35 to 6.65 m.** Fitting a level eave to the photo's edge through (0.063, 0.075) and (0.104, 0.095) gives 6.6 m. The soffit is black near the camera and lit warm toward the far overhang (cells along v 0.11 to 0.16), so it is three boxes.
- **The annex's far end is plaster.** Only its top half metre under the lean-to eave is light (photo u 0.25 to 0.29, v 0.50 to 0.545); below that is brown board, and the far lower wall is in deep shadow. The far street's left third (u 0.33 to 0.38) stays in the light while its middle is in shadow.
- **A koshi lattice can be balanced by area.** From the photo camera the far lattice is seen at a grazing angle where only slats show, so its wall is balanced for a 0.7 visible-slat fraction, the near one for 0.5.
- **Slab tops can share the mortar's surface.** They z-fought with it and the treads read darker; the mortar body now sits 3 cm below every slab top.
- **The corner house at the bend can start where the far street ends.** Its front stood 1.2 m in front of the street's last slabs and the paving passed through it; it now stands behind the street's end, the street runs on behind it, and its left edge stops at photo u 0.37 where the photo shows paving.
- **A block placed by the photo is placed.** The critic's raycasts showed both sliding doors, a lantern and the awning built inside the wall boxes they belonged to (the left row's "in front" is +x, and the offsets were written for the right house's -x), the sign floating beyond house 3's end, and the side stair's top three treads inside the planter's core. The compare scores cannot see any of that: a door inside a wall scores exactly like the wall.
- **A point on a photo ray stays put when its wall moves.** The sign is placed by the ray through (0.33, 0.58) at a chosen x; setting house 3's wall back 0.7 m and moving that x with it slid the sign 2.5 m down the street into open air. It now keeps its original x and hangs from a bracket arm off a post at the wall's corner.
- **The black block at the top-left was invisible.** A probe of four corner pixels hit roof tiles, but the cell mean was dark because of it; removing it cost 0.44 in that cell. It is now a dormer rising from the mezzanine's top through the roof's edge, a wedge with a boarded front and its own tiled shed roof.
- **The white awning is a separate hood.** It is a light sheet lying on the door canopy's lower rows (photo u 0.13 to 0.20, v 0.50 to 0.57). Shortening the canopy to start beyond it removed the light roof plane the photo shows at u 0.06 to 0.13 and cost 0.002 of cell distance. As a `quadSlab` with a textured material the sheet first rendered only its underside (the corner order winds the top face downward and `surface()` is single-sided); it is now two-sided and 10 cm above the tiles, clear of their crests.
- **The annex wall reaches its roof's eave.** Raising it above the door canopy showed a brown band where the photo has the canopy's light tiles: the canopy plane rises behind the wall face, so the wall stops just under the canopy and the view over its top meets tiles. Only the far section (z < -10.2) rises through the canopy to carry the plaster band, with a tile-colored ledge over its top.
- **The annex runs on under its roofs.** Its lean-to roof and door canopy run 1.9 to 2.2 m past the terrace's end over house 3's near part. Carrying the wall on to the roofs' end costs 0.0005 of cell distance and 0.004 of SSIM, because the photo shows house 3's roof and plaster below the canopy's far tiles at u 0.27 to 0.31; even the lower boxes alone cost more. The overhang stays.
- **House 1's mezzanine wall should run under its whole roof.** The photo shows sky and the mountains under the roof's far overhang (u 0.17 to 0.28, v 0.12 to 0.25); a wall or gable there would cover them. The wall ends at z = -7.5 and the soffit boxes carry the overhang.
- **A darker eave underside is truer.** The photo's eave band is brownish over most of its length; the sampled band mean on the hisashi's board and rafters scored 0.0007 better than the dark wall color.
- **The low wall's cap is a row of tiles.** From the photo camera it is a flat light band; half-round caps, then a full gabled cap in the tile mean, both scored worse than no cap. The cap is now low-profile pan tiles and small ridge tiles in the wall's own light mean, stepped along the wall's length rather than its z projection, which reads flat from the photo view and as tiles up close.

## How it was checked

Every landmark position was probed (`npm run probe`) after each module landed, the worst cells were ranked from `out/scores.json` after each change, photo-versus-render crops settled the ambiguous cells, and views (three orbit angles, five close-ups of my own, thirteen of the first critic's, 42 of the second's, the third's own) were captured through the live page and inspected at native size, with the critics' raycast lists (26, then 90) rerun at the current positions after each fix, including rays at the fence corners that name which piece must be on top. A bisect script applied each suspect change alone and scored it; that is what separated the fixes the photo disliked (canopy shortened, dark eave underside, tall wall cap, roof extended over house 3, annex carried past its terrace) from the ones it was indifferent to (fence jog, dormer, stone inset, lattice window). In the close-ups tiles, caps, ridges, rafters, slats, shoji bars, rail posts, slabs with joints, stacked stones with mortar, doors, lanterns and the scalloped noren are all geometry; the remaining non-geometry are the phase 3 placeholders (blob vegetation, floating far-roof cards) and the corner house at the bend.

## Bounds worth knowing

- Balancing (`balancedMean`) assumes the visible fraction of the accent element from the photo camera; another camera sees a different mix, which is fine for a scene but means the balanced wall colors are not the photo's wall colors.
- The mean correction shifts the whole texture, so a strongly patterned texture (the noren) ends up with a lifted white to pay for its dark crest.
- The far street's slabs stop at z = -42; the bend beyond is unpaved ground.
- The side stair climbs 1.9 m from a plinth 1.5 m above the street to the planter's top, and its plinth stands 0.27 m into the street over the main treads' right edge: those are the phase 1 photo fit, not a plausible flight, and the fence's jog is what lets all its treads exist. The photo's own flight is small and half hidden; phase 3's vegetation may cover part of it.
- House 1's hisashi and the annex roof both have their eaves behind their wall faces (the photo pins the eave lines at u < 0.18 and u < 0.27); the walls' top strips under them are covered by the canopy or a ledge, and the eave undersides show beyond the ground floor's far end.
- House 1's top roof overhangs 4.5 m beyond its mezzanine wall's far end, and the annex's roofs overhang 2 m past the annex over house 3: both because the photo shows sky, or house 3, under them. The soffit boxes carry the first; nothing carries the second.
- The lanterns hang by the entrance behind the photo's left edge; the photo's own lanterns are dark boxes under the far eaves.
- The compare scores cannot see placement inside geometry. A probe gate that asserts the first hit under each landmark is queued in `docs/learning/lessons.md`; until it lands, the check is a raycast list rerun at the current positions.
