# 2026-09-17 — the environment map is proved instead of assumed, and one sub-hypothesis is dead

`src/lighting.js` built an image-based light out of the sky dome, handed it to every lit material, and never looked at it. That is the hole the unexplained frame of this morning fell through: `npm run shot` drew `0e56fc677faf` scoring 0.0632 / 0.5718 from a tree that scored 0.05958 / 0.57134 in three other runs, and the previous lane measured the difference between those two frames to be, to the precision the recorded cell means carry, this map's contribution and nothing else — 416 of 528 cells land on the bad frame EXACTLY when the environment is removed, against 63 for the good frame and 68 for the next best of twelve scene knobs (`docs/devlog/detailed/2026-09-17-shot-rung.md`). It recommended proving the map and could not, because `src/lighting.js` was outside its files.

This is that change. The short version:

| | what it does | red-proved | cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| the map's own radiance | renders the PMREM through a detector and reads its mean luma and non-finite texels | yes, a black sky dome reads 0 against 117.6 | 12 ms on SwiftShader |
| the texture's shape | the fields three compiles the cube-UV lookup from, at build and re-read live | yes, height 0 and a height that changes after the build | free |
| the per-material binding | which environment each lit program was compiled against | yes, a stale record on one of 239, and a run that measured none | free (0.13 ms per `describe()`) |
| **what the map adds to the frame** | the photo view at 64x64 with the map's intensity on and off | yes, in isolation, at `RIG.envIntensity = 0.001` | two 64x64 renders |
| the rebuild | a bad map is built once more before it is installed | yes, a first build that is black and a second that is not | 0 unless it fires |

**The contract frame did not move, on either renderer.** On SwiftShader `out/render.png` is sha256 `ef7a32617aa6f91f…` and `0.05714567082372411 / 0.585612723403553` before the change and after it — the full-precision pair was read off the BEFORE `out/scores.json`, on the before tree `ef9ad4798a5efec4`, and again off the after one. On the GPU, `npm run try` run A/B/A/B with only these two source files swapped gives frame sha256 `4ade9bba0ba03416` in all four arms. And on seven cameras: `npm run views` run on main's three source files and on this lane's gives the same seven pose digests and the same sweep `ad9ae6e9f117`. `npm test` PASSES on the handoff tree `af3550c0d20889fc`, ten gates, exit 0.

## The instrument first, and what it settled

`out/scratch/env-probe.mjs` measures the map and the three sub-hypotheses' fields on a live page, on both renderers, before any source was changed. Three of its readings decided the design.

**`scene.environmentIntensity` is a uniform and `scene.environment` is a define.** Setting the intensity to 0 leaves `renderer.info.programs` at 44 and costs 9 ms on an RTX 4090 and 428 ms on SwiftShader; setting `scene.environment` to null takes the program count to 62 and costs 1177 ms and 1675 ms. The two answers differ by 0.016 of a luma level. So the contribution is measured through the intensity: same number, no recompile, and a measurement that costs a recompile is one nobody would keep in `buildLighting`.

**The healthy figures, and they differ by renderer, so nothing near them is pinned.** The map's mean luma over 1024 taps: **115.6** on the GPU, **117.6** on SwiftShader. The contribution at the photo view on a warmed page: **2.768** and **2.812** luma levels — the same size as the **2.9** that separates the flake (referenceLuma 54.6) from the contract (57.5). Measured inside `buildLighting`, where the camera is square and the shadow flags have not been applied yet, it is **2.64** on the GPU and **2.69** on SwiftShader, and those are the figures a gate prints. 239 of 256 standard materials have a program at the photo view and all 239 are bound to the map in place; the other 17 have never been drawn and have no program to ask about, which is why the check is "none disagrees" rather than "all 256 agree".

## What the proof is, and why that shape

Four measurements, because there were three named sub-hypotheses and a proof that says only "no light" cannot tell them apart — and because the whole reason this lane exists is that the real mechanism is still unknown, so one END-TO-END number is worth more than three mechanism-specific ones.

1. **The map's radiance.** `inspectEnvMap` renders the PMREM through the same detector shader `src/post.js` puts on the composer's buffers and reads back the mean luminance and any texel that is not a finite number. A black PMREM reads 0.
2. **The texture's shape.** `describeEnvTexture` reads the mapping, the type and `image.width/height`, which is where three gets `envMapCubeUVHeight` from. Free.
3. **The per-material binding.** `envBinding` counts the lit materials whose recorded environment is not the one on the scene. Free, no render.
4. **What the map adds to the frame.** `envContribution` renders the photo view at 64x64 with no post chain, once at `RIG.envIntensity` and once at 0.

The first and the fourth run once, at build. The second and third are re-read on every `envState()`, along with "is `scene.environment` still the map this rig installed" and "is the intensity still the rig's". `describe().env` carries all of it, `tools/shot.js` records, prints and refuses it, `tools/compare.js` refuses the sidecar.

**Measurement 3 is narrower than it looks, and reading three settled why.** `materialProperties.environment` is written in exactly one place, the program-build path (`three.module.js:18167` at the pinned 0.185.1), and it is not one of the fields the `needsProgramChange` chain tests — that chain tests `materialProperties.envMap` (`:18442`). Two consequences. three never repairs a stale value by itself, which is why the forced-stale arm below stays red for the rest of the page. And every NATURAL way `scene.environment` can change also changes `envMap`, which does force a rebuild and resyncs the field — so the case measurement 3 was meant to catch is caught by the identity check beside it, and the only thing that makes the count fire alone is a write into three's own record. It is kept because it is free and because it is the only check here that looks at the compiled programs rather than at the scene; it is not claimed to be load-bearing.

### Why a rebuild, and why not a throw

**A bad MAP is built again, once, before it is installed.** The dome is deterministic and the generator is fresh, so a second build is a real remedy for something transient and costs nothing on a machine where the first one worked. Proved twice over: with the first build forced black and the second left alone, the rebuilt map reads 117.6 and the contribution reads 2.69 — the healthy figures exactly — and two runs of the hunt that landed inside that mutation window recorded `builds: 2, ok: true` and drew **`ef7a32617aa6`, the byte-identical contract frame**. A rebuild that works really is the same map.

**A map that is fine and whose light does not arrive is reported and NOT rebuilt.** Rebuilding an already-good map cannot fix a lookup, a binding or an unknown fourth mechanism, and it would hide the fault behind a second identical map.

**Nothing throws and nothing is switched off.** The page is a sandbox a person opens and a scene that is 5% dark is still a scene; the frame that must not be wrong is the scored one. That is the split `src/post.js` settled on for its watchdog — announce, record, and let the gate whose frame is a contract be the one that fails.

**A rebuild that then worked still fails `npm run shot`,** and that is the one refusal here that is about the machine rather than the pixels — the hunt rows above show the frame is identical. It is refused because a first build carrying no light is the 2026-09-17 event happening in front of us, and a green run would bury it in a log nobody reads. It costs one re-run. The counter-argument was `src/post.js`'s own `falseAlarms`, which deliberately does NOT fail `shot`; the difference is that a false alarm is a KNOWN-noisy instrument (a default-framebuffer read) while this verdict comes from `readRenderTargetPixels` on the map itself.

## What was believed and disproved

**"Setting `material.envMapIntensity = 0` will isolate the contribution."** It does nothing at all: 57.09 before, 57.09 with every one of the 256 lit materials zeroed, 57.09 after two more renders. `three.module.js:18690` writes `envMapIntensity` into the uniform from `scene.environmentIntensity` on every `setProgram`, ungated, so a per-material value written from outside never survives a draw. The isolating mutation had to be `RIG.envIntensity` itself.

**"A texture whose `image.height` is 0 is a SILENT black, which is why it needs a field check."** It is not silent on this renderer. three writes the mip count into every lit program as `${Math.log2(height) - 2}.0`, so a height of 0 emits `-Infinity.0` and **18 fragment shaders fail to compile**, each one a `console.error` — and `tools/shot.js` already fails on any console error. So the field check's value here is the diagnosis (one sentence naming `envMapCubeUVHeight`) rather than the catch.

**And that kills sub-hypothesis 2 for the 2026-09-17 frame.** That run completed and wrote `out/render.png`, and `shot` fails on any console error, so the page had none. A zero-height environment texture cannot have been what drew it. What would have to be true for that conclusion to be wrong: that a height of 0 can reach the shader without a compile error on some other driver, or that the flake's height was not 0 but some other wrong value — which is the next paragraph, and is not covered.

**"A wrong-but-positive height would be the silent variant, and the contribution check will catch it."** Measured, and wrong twice over. With `image.height` forced to 4 at build, `npm run shot` **passed, exit 0** — the lookup reads the wrong texels rather than none, and the contribution read **3.27** against SwiftShader's healthy 2.69, i.e. the frame got MORE light, not less. So: a wrong height baked in at build is caught by nothing here, and it is not necessarily a loss of light at all. What the shape check does cover is a height of 0 and a shape that CHANGES after the build, and that second branch exists because of this measurement.

**"three repairs a stale `materialProperties.environment` on the next draw, so the binding check can never fire in practice."** Measured: a record forced stale on one of the 239 was still reported one frame later, and for the rest of the page. The first draft explained that by frustum culling — the build-time render compiles at aspect 1 and the shot is 1200/1100 — and an independent review showed that cannot be the mechanism, because aspect 1 is the NARROWER frustum and its draw set is a subset. The real reason is in three's source, above: the field is written on program build and never tested for invalidation.

## The red proofs

Every one is in `docs/learning/gate-proofs.md` with its output and the renderer it ran on. Four are source mutations driven through `npm run shot` itself, not through a scratch reimplementation of its path; five are page states no source edit can hold still, driven through the real `describe().env` on a real page, each from a green control and back to a green control.

The pair worth reading together is R1 and R3, because they are the discrimination working:

- **R1, a black sky dome.** Map mean luma **0**, contribution **0**, `builds` 2. Three reasons.
- **R3, `RIG.envIntensity = 0.001`.** Map mean luma **117.6**, shape healthy, binding clean, intensity matching the rig — and the contribution **0.02** against a floor of 0.25. Exactly one reason.

R3 also red-proves the ABSOLUTE half of the contribution floor. The floor is `max(0.25, 4 x RIG.envIntensity)`: the proportional half tracks the rig so nobody maintains a figure, and at `RIG.envIntensity = 0.001` it would be 0.004 and would pass a scene with no environment light at all. 0.25 is what makes a floor built out of the very constant it checks non-vacuous, and R3 is the mutation that reaches it.

R2 is the same discrimination from the other side: a map reading a healthy 117.6 whose light does not arrive (contribution 0). Its exit 1 is over-determined — the same run also printed `FAIL: 18 page error(s)` from the pre-existing console-error check — and the gate-proofs table says so.

## The hunt, and it did not fire

`out/scratch/hunt.mjs` is `shot`'s own path with the screenshot hashed in memory instead of written, one fresh node process and one fresh chromium per run, appending a JSON line carrying the frame's digest, the source tree, the ready time and the whole of `describe().env` and `describe().post`. It ran beside this work.

**Its first 17 runs proved nothing and it took a look at the log to notice.** Ten of them died on `page.screenshot: Timeout 30000ms exceeded` — `tools/shot.js` sets `page.setDefaultTimeout(ACTION_TIMEOUT_MS)` and the first version of this script did not, so under load the screenshot exceeded Playwright's default. A hunt that cannot tell "did not reproduce" from "did not run" is worth nothing, and this one was two thirds the second thing. Those rows are kept at `out/scratch/hunt-broken-timeout.jsonl` and are not counted.

After the fix: **37 rows, 0 failed.** 35 of them on the clean tree `198b579904a53f9c`, every one `ef7a32617aa6`, the contract frame; the other 2 landed inside the R4 mutation window and are the `builds: 2` rows quoted above. Every clean row reads `env ok=true, map 117.6, height 1024, mismatched 0, contribution 2.69, builds 1` and `referenceLuma 57.6` — one distinct reading across all 35. Ready times 28.5 to 57.5 s, with no fast outlier like the 13.7 s and 14.5 s of the two known bad events, though this box was at 100% CPU throughout so everything here is slower than those.

**Two caveats on the count, both against it.** The 30-run loop overlapped with about 7 runs of the earlier loop that survived being stopped — six consecutive rows are seconds apart — so this is not 37 runs of one sequential loop and the load profile is not the one that reproduced the earlier watchdog flake. And the tree guard reads the source hash once, before the browser starts, unlike `tools/shot.js` which re-reads it at the end; a run that started clean and loaded a mutated page would be compared against the contract digest and reported as a flake. Neither happened here — the two mutation-window rows were correctly marked `unchecked` — but the guard is weaker than its comment.

Fifty-six fresh `shot` processes across the previous lane's three loops had already failed to reproduce the frame, so not reproducing it was the expected outcome and the proof is the deliverable.

## Three findings the file boundary had excluded, and the coordinator widened the lane for them

The review's last three findings sat in files this lane was not allowed to touch. The coordinator widened the scope for exactly those three and nothing else, on the reasoning that routing them elsewhere would hand someone else this lane's context for a few lines of change. All three are red-proved in `docs/learning/gate-proofs.md` and all three landed before the suite ran, so the suite ran on the tree that was handed off.

**`tools/lib/browser.js` — and this one is not polish.** `collectErrors` returned early unless a console line began `post:` or `watchdog:`, so `src/lighting.js`'s `env:` warning would have been printed by no gate at all. Shipping a proof whose warning nothing listens to would have recreated, for the light, the exact defect this repo had already paid to find for the post chain earlier the same day: a step-down announced with `console.warn` that `collectErrors` never looked at, so every gate passed silently on a degraded page. It is the same shape and it is fixed the same way. Two `page: env:` lines are printed with the prefix in the list and zero without it, `npm run placement` in both arms.

**It is ECHOED and deliberately NOT COLLECTED, and that fork is worth writing down because the next person to add a warning prefix will face it.** `collectErrors` keeps a second list, `postWarnings`, which feeds one sentence in `shot`: "the page announced N post-chain step-down(s)". Routing an `env:` line into that list would have made `shot` print a count of something that did not happen, and a refusal that reports the wrong cause is worse than the gap it closes. The state is the right channel because all three refusing tools read it: `shot`, `compare` and `try`. Echo everything a person should see; collect only what the sentence at the other end is actually about.

**`tools/try.js`.** It already refused a frame the post chain did not draw as designed, on the grounds that two arms made by different machinery cannot be compared. A lost environment is the same fault and a bigger one: 0.0036 of cell distance is 36 times that tool's own margin, so an iteration lane would have read a broken instrument as a real improvement and chased it — the exact failure the margin exists to prevent. Its calibration and its printed margin figures are untouched.

**`src/post.js` — the same bug this lane's own review had just caught in `src/lighting.js`.** `watchPostChain` saved `renderer.info.render` across its verification and restored the whole object, `frame` included. That field is three's once-per-frame memo key for geometry, video-texture and UBO uploads, so the restore rolled it back and the next few real frames could skip an upload in silence. It is bookkeeping in appearance and a scene change in fact, so it is held to the scene-change bar: after it the contract frame is still `ef7a32617aa6f91f…` at 0.0571 / 0.5856 on SwiftShader and `4ade9bba0ba03416` on the GPU. That is also why the full suite runs after these three and not before — `animation`, `nudge` and `record` are the three gates that render repeatedly, and a per-frame counter fault is exactly the kind of thing a single frame cannot see.

**And the lesson the blocker carries beyond this lane.** Requiring the new environment block of every scene would have reddened `SCENE=whitehouse npm test` on a scene this lane never touched and that belongs to another agent — a check of ours breaking someone else's gate from inside a file we share. **A check added while working on one scene must be asked whether it is a rule about the repo or a rule about that scene.** This one looked like the first and was the second: the proof lives in `src/lighting.js`, which is scene 1's rig, and every other scene has its own folder, its own rig and its own `describe()`. The rule it turned into is "required of the default scene, checked wherever it is present".

## What the suite covered that a byte-identical frame could not

`npm test` PASSES on the handoff tree, ten gates, exit 0: `import-inert` 5 s, `shot` 64 s, `compare` 1 s, `placement` 12 s, `namerules` 11 s, `clearance` 39 s, `animation` 11 s, `nudge` 17 s, `blackframe` 45 s, `record` 26 s, then the thresholds — cell distance 0.0571 against 0.0586 and SSIM 0.5856 against 0.5806. The box was at 100% CPU from other repos' sessions throughout, so those seconds are upper bounds and not a budget.

Running it was not a formality, and the reason is the `src/post.js` fix. That counter is a memo key for uploads, and the argument for the whole change is "no pixel moves" — evidence for which, until the suite, was **one frame at one clock from one camera**. A single frame cannot see a fault whose whole nature is that it affects the frames AFTER it. Three gates can:

- **`animation`** renders the photo view at seven points across the animation cycle and scores the worst of them. It is the only scored gate that advances the clock, so a frame that drifts once the scene has been running — which is exactly what a skipped geometry or instance-matrix upload would look like — lands here and nowhere else.
- **`nudge`** renders three poses TWICE each and requires the two renders to be bit-identical before it moves the camera 2 mm. That identical-output half is the direct question "does the second render of the same thing differ from the first", which a gate that renders once cannot ask at all.
- **`record`** drives the camera with real mouse events and checks every one of 963 frames over twelve seconds of real time. It is the only gate that exercises the controls rather than posing the camera and the only one that samples continuously instead of at moments, so a degradation that appears after some frames, or only while something is being dragged, is visible to it alone. Worst frame: 3 of 25 probes dark, limit 12.

## What is now known, and what is not

**Known.**

- The environment map is measured on every page load, on both renderers, and the numbers are recorded beside every scored frame. A map that fails at build is now red on its own run, which `compare`'s same-tree light check cannot be on the first run of a fresh tree.
- Sub-hypothesis 2 (a zero-height texture) is **ruled out** for that frame, by the shader-compile measurement above.
- Sub-hypothesis 1 (a black PMREM) and 3 (a stale binding) are not ruled out, and both are now one field of one JSON line away from being confirmed or excluded the next time it happens.

**Not known.** The mechanism. It did not reproduce here and did not in 56 fresh `shot` processes before. Nothing measured on either renderer, across every run in this lane, has ever shown the map reading anything but 115.6 / 117.6 with a contribution of 2.6 to 2.8 and zero mismatched bindings. **Nothing here establishes that this proof would have caught the 2026-09-17 frame**: the map and the contribution are measured once, at build, so any mechanism that removes the environment term afterwards without moving the texture's identity, its shape, the intensity or the binding reads as proved and scores.

**The bounds of what was added**, stated where the checks live and repeated here because they are the next lane's starting point:

- The map's radiance and the contribution are measured ONCE, at build. There is no watchdog for the light the way there is one for the post chain, and adding one means a per-frame hook in `src/main.js`.
- The shape check covers a height of 0 and a shape that changes after the build. A wrong height present at build is covered by nothing, measured.
- Measurement 3 reads a field three writes on program build and never tests for invalidation, so its natural case is covered by the identity check beside it.
- It proves the map carries light and reaches the materials. It does not prove the map is RIGHT: a sky rebuilt in the wrong colour passes every check here.
- Every gate now ECHOES an `env:` warning, and three REFUSE on the state: `shot`, `compare` and `try`. Every other gate prints the line and goes on scoring its own question, which is deliberate rather than an oversight.
- `describe().env` costs 0.1285 ms on the GPU, measured over 200 calls. Every gate calls `describe()` once or twice; `tools/record.js` calls it once per recorded frame, so it adds about 0.8% of that gate's 16 ms frame budget.
