## A. The hill's near face (brief priority 1)

**The one axis the photo cannot see.** Every vertex of `hill()` is placed by `L.uvToWorld(u, v, depth)`, so moving a vertex in DEPTH slides it along its own ray through the photo camera and leaves its photo position where it was. That is what lets the hill get spurs and gullies for free: the silhouette, the skyline and the texture mapping are all functions of (u, v). It is not bit-identical, because UV interpolation inside a triangle is perspective-correct and a vertex that moves in depth shifts the texture inside its own triangle — measured below at two cells out of 528.

Four things were built and three of them had to be re-measured after a first version moved scored cells.

1. **Relief**, a depth field of three octaves at plus or minus 46 m on the largest, ramped in from a quarter of the way down each column and out again at 80%. The ramp's parameter is the column's OWN ridge-to-foot height, not a distance in frame. A fixed 0.09 of frame was the first version: the hill's skyline is at v 0.10 where the photo frame is and at v 0.94 at its left end, so one frame-distance is a third of the way down the hill in the photo and past its foot in the sweep. It cost **+0.0518 over the eight cells at v 0.205** (+0.0550 over the four largest). A first draft of this line said "0.043 over five"; re-derived from `cells-base` against `cells-a1`.
2. **Rows**, 12 to 30. `rows` is not free in the scored frame even where the geometry does not move: 30 EVEN rows measured **+0.0343 at the hill cell (0.771, 0.159) and +0.0473 over the eight cells at v 0.205** (+0.0492 over the four largest), on a tree where every one of those cells has relief ramp exactly 0. A first draft said "+0.029 over four"; the +0.0343 reproduces exactly and the other figure did not. The original twelve row positions are kept to the bit and the extra rows inserted only below band 3.
3. **Shading**, and this is the part that was wrong twice. It is a vertex-colour multiplier from `n . sun` and `n.y`, and the BASELINE has to be the same vertex's normal on the UNDISPLACED surface. A mesh-wide mean shifts every row near the ridge — the only rows the photo frame sees. A per-ROW mean is not zero either, because a row of this mesh runs at a fixed fraction of each column's own ridge-to-foot height and so follows the skyline's shape; that version left the same cells 0.0343 and 0.0473 worse with the relief at 0 under all of them. With the flat-normal baseline, **the whole change moves 2 cells of 528, net -0.001** — and those two cells are (0.688, 0.432) and (0.688, 0.477), which is NOT the perspective-correct UV shift a first draft attributed them to: see the critic's finding 1 below.
4. **Haze and a body tone.** The body tone is gated on the quarter-of-a-column line; the HAZE IS NOT — it is a function of distance from the photo camera alone, and a first draft of this line said both shared the gate. It is safe for a different reason: the farthest point of any hill triangle overlapping the photo frame is 642.4 m against a 700 m ramp start. The hill spans 4.6 frame widths; the part the scored frame sees is 490 to 620 m away and its left end is 1200 m, and one flat `hillDeep` covered the lot.

Then the texture: the crown grid is 571 cells per FRAME width and this texture spans 4.6 frames at 4096, so **a crown is 1.56 texels** — below Nyquist, which is why the hill reads as sandpaper from a pose that fills a third of the screen with it and as forest in the photo's 0.02 of frame. Damping the crown field to 0.68 and raising the two larger scales traded speckle for structure and **gained 0.0000163 of cell distance and 0.0000123 of SSIM** (hill -0.0142 over its six cells, blossoms +0.0067). A first draft printed 0.0163 and 0.0001 — the first is a thousandfold overstatement and more than six times the whole iteration's gain. The parenthetical is right and is what identifies the arm; `out/scratch/ARMS.md` now labels every arm file so a reader can re-derive any of these.

Last, the skyline past u 1.05. It ran straight from (0.95, 0.075) to (1.6, 0.07) — 0.6 of a frame width as one ruled line, which is the hard straight silhouette every sweep review since iteration 2 has named. Four secondary summits, only ever RAISING the line so the mountain cards' gap widens rather than narrows. **Identical to the digit on `npm run try`.**

## What the critic found

One independent read-only critic on the diff and the claims, asked why the numbers do not support them. It wrote its own probes (`out/scratch/critic/`) rather than re-running mine, and measured on tree `adf14a06d6ad4291`. It found **one materially false claim, three published figures that do not reproduce, four qualifications, and eight claims it checked and found sound**. All of the below is fixed in what ships.

**1. "The hill's relief and shading cannot reach a scored cell" is FALSE.** Of 528 cell rays, 391 reach `hill`; 33 of those have nothing OPAQUE in front (the cherry's cards are alpha-tested); **11 of those land on a face this iteration displaced and shaded**. The deepest is (0.688, 0.432) at **t = 0.368**, where the relief ramp is 0.881 and `hillBody` is mixed in at 0.341 — 2.2x the "t = 0.165" I published and well past the 0.25 gate. Where my figure came from: 0.165 is exactly cell (0.729, 0.250), the deepest cell whose FIRST hit is hill-or-blossom-over-hill **at v <= 0.25**, and I read that as the deepest cell the hill reaches at all.
