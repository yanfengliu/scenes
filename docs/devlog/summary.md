- **2026-09-06 — The blackouts were NaN, and the size decided it.** A user reported "giant rectangular blackouts" that survived the camera stopping and cleared on a reset. The camera was incidental: with the camera still, at a 1280x720 window and a device pixel ratio of 1.5 (a 1920x1080 drawing buffer, a 2304x1296 composer) the frame is 96.6% near black through the post chain and 0.8% through `renderer.render` with no chain at all. Read back as half floats, both composer buffers are 100% NaN, and NaN reaches the canvas as black. Swept in hundredths from 1.00 to 1.60 times the drawing buffer, 14 of 61 sizes fail at 8 samples and 10 of 61 at 4, the sets only partly overlapping, deterministic to two decimals in both directions, with `gl.getError()` 0 and the framebuffer complete throughout. Turning the bloom off fixes every one; strength 0, threshold 1e6 and radius 0 do not, so the bloom spreads the NaN and does not make it. Zero samples fixes every one. The scene is clean in a fresh target of the same size either way. Two tempting fixes were killed by measurement: it is not a broken multisample *resolve* (a bright gradient painted through the same target, and the whole chain run on a synthetic input, come back perfect at every bad size, which is also why no synthetic probe can stand in for the frame), and it cannot be routed around a buffer (giving the scene its own multisampled target and leaving the composer's plain still produced the NaN). So `src/post.js` now proves its own output: on build and on every size change it renders one real frame, samples both buffers for anything that is not a finite number, weighs the frame against the same view rendered at 64x64 with no chain, and steps down a ladder — half the multisamples, a neighbouring size, no supersample, no multisampling — warning in the console each time, and starting again from the top at the next size so no machine is held to a lesser chain. Dragged across 56 window widths at a device pixel ratio of 1.5, not one frame is black: 37 sizes keep all eight multisamples and 19 fall to four, and nothing loses multisampling or the supersample. The worst frame in the sweep is 1.98% near black, which is the normal figure. `npm run blackframe` is the new gate: six size and ratio combinations including non-round ratios, each measured on load and after a resize, seen red at four of twelve rows against the old code. Note what was blind: `npm run nudge`, written for this same user's previous report, scores how much the frame *changes*, and a black frame is perfectly stable, so it passed this catastrophe for its whole life. Nothing else moved: cell distance 0.0811 and SSIM 0.4411 to the digit, nudge 0.145% and 0.253% to the digit, animation identical across all seven frames, 316 draw calls and 5.3 to 5.4 ms median at 1920x1080. The check costs one frame per size change and nothing per frame.
