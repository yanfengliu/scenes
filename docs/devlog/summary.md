# Devlog summary

One short dated line per behaviour-changing session, newest first. History, not status. Anything a later session could trip over is in `detailed/`.

- **2026-09-06 — A scene picker, and one box for the chrome.** The repository is meant to hold many scenes and had one page wired to the first, so there is now a registry (`src/scenes.js`: id, title, entry module), a boot seam (`src/boot.js`, which `index.html` loads instead of `src/main.js`: it resolves `?scene=<id>`, sets the tab title, mounts the picker and imports that scene's entry) and the dropdown itself (`src/picker.js`), which lists one scene today. Choosing one reloads the page rather than swapping in place, so no scene has to know how to tear itself down, and the default scene drops the query instead of pinning it. Adding scene 2 is a folder under `src/` and an entry in `SCENES`; scene 1's modules stay flat in `src/` because moving them would rewrite every tool import for no gain. The trap it leaves: the scored image is a screenshot of the page, so chrome outside the new `#hud` box will be in it — `shot` and `animation` each carried their own copy of the hiding CSS, and both now import `HIDE_UI_CSS` from `tools/lib/browser.js`, which hides `#hud` whole. The HUD spans the window so a long scene name wraps, which means `pointer-events: none` on the box and `auto` on its children or it eats every drag along the bottom edge. `R` with the dropdown focused no longer resets the camera, and neither does Ctrl+R. Scores unchanged at 0.0811 and 0.4411. See `detailed/2026-09-06-scene-picker.md`.

- **2026-09-06 — The CI ceiling, measured.** The run for the flicker commit 15692d1 was cancelled at sixty minutes and nineteen seconds, partway through `nudge`, and a cancelled run reads like a failure without being one: every gate it reached matched local exactly (0.0811, 0.4411, 19 placement and 8 grounding checks, animation inside every bound), and 8309db9, which contains 15692d1, then went green in 46 minutes with the full suite, its nudge figures matching local digit for digit at 0.145% and 0.253%. So the fix is certified on the remote gate and nothing about the scene was wrong. `timeout-minutes` is now 90. The numbers behind it, since the first attempt guessed and blamed the wrong gate: locally, under the same SwiftShader the runner uses, animation at three frames takes 354 s and nudge 136 s; the runner ran about seven times slower under four competing runs, which puts the suite at roughly 66 minutes there, of which animation is 41, nudge 16 and everything else 9. Free runners do it in 46. The animation gate, not nudge, is the one to trim if this is ever tight again, and it is already down to three frames on CI. Workflows run at the version in the commit under test, so any commit at or below 15692d1 still carries the old ceiling and can be cut off the same way; a `workflow_dispatch` on current main is the way to certify the tree instead.

- **2026-09-05 — Phase 5 landed: life, interaction, performance, delivery.** The scene moves: wind through the canopy and the noren, 420 petals falling, the clouds drifting, the lanterns swinging, all in the vertex shaders from one deterministic clock the gates can pin (`src/animation.js`). A new gate scores seven frames across the cycle, because a gate that shoots one frame cannot see a drift at t = 3 s; the spread is 0.0001 of cell distance and 0.0005 of SSIM. The grounding lesson landed as the second half of `tools/placement.js` and is retired with its proof. Controls gained a floor and corridor clamp, touch bindings, a pixel-ratio cap and a loading indicator; delivery gained a README, `docs/render.webp`, a Pages workflow and a CI workflow that runs the gates on SwiftShader and does not assert the perf budget. Two measured wins closed the phase: the sky's blue taken from the photo's own top row, and the landing shading into the bend. A user reported the scene flickering while they moved the camera; it was geometry thinner than a pixel, not the depth fighting first suspected, and the fix is multisampling plus a 1.2x resolve, gated by `npm run nudge` at two device pixel ratios and recorded in the defect register. Cell distance 0.0820 to 0.0811, SSIM 0.4338 to 0.4411, 316 draw calls, 3.2 ms median. The critic caught the new gate passing a fourteen-fold canopy sway, so it now bounds the motion between frames as well as the scores, and has been seen red. See `detailed/2026-09-05-phase-5.md`.

- **2026-09-05 — Phase 4 landed: sky, light, atmosphere, post.** The scene is lit: a rig of neutral ambient, a sky tint, the low sun with capped shadow casters and a PMREM environment from the sky itself, the parked normal and roughness maps in use, a rewritten sky dome with cirrus and cumulus, and a composer with bloom, a vignette and one ACES pass at the end. The decision the phase turns on: the sampled means stay the target displayed color, and each material's albedo is the radiance that displays as that mean once the rig and the tone curve have had their say (`src/tonemap.js` inverts the curve three applies). Cell distance 0.0844 to 0.0825, SSIM 0.4159 to 0.4301, 316 draw calls for the whole frame, 3.4 ms median. Three things proved false: the port of the tone curve (three divides by 0.6 where the port multiplied), that a blank frame meant a broken post chain (hand-built geometries had no normals, and one NaN spreads through the bloom blur), and that bloom is a finishing touch (its first settings cost more than the entire lighting rig). The canopy now reaches over the machiya's eave, which was a carried gap; see `detailed/2026-09-05-phase-4.md`.

- **2026-09-05 — Phase 3 landed: vegetation, figures, background.** The weeping cherry as spline limbs, 760 hanging strands and about 30,700 instanced blossom cards colored from a baked field of the photo's own cell means (`photofield.js`), four pines of needle cards on the hillside beyond the bend, shrubs, potted plants, moss and weeds as cards, a low-poly person, gabled far houses on ground strips, a hill surface with cellular tree crowns and two sampled mountain layers, all through one material factory (`materials.js`) with a lit flag for phase 4. Cell distance 0.0899 to 0.0844, SSIM 0.3273 to 0.4159, 211 draw calls, 1.3 ms median. A placement gate (`tools/placement.js`, in `npm test`) now asserts the first mesh under 19 landmarks, retiring the phase 2 lesson. Two critic rounds: the first found the pines standing in the far street, a limb rising into the sky, stems past their blossoms and floating far houses; the second found that grounding them had put a far house's light wall in the street and the new ground strips in frame, and left the shrub in the fence roof. All fixed. Things proved false: pink alone does not find the blossom (the sun-lit top and the lavender strand ends needed their own rules), the plan's evergreen box starts too high, photo positions at a chosen depth do not make ground, and phase 2's longer stone wall was hiding the red door; see `detailed/2026-09-05-phase-3.md`.

- **2026-09-05 — Phase 2 landed: architecture and stone.** The block-out split into modules (`scene.js` assembles `background`, `paving`, `walls`, `roofs`, `facades`, `architecture`, `vegetation`, `figures`), with mean-corrected canvas textures (`textures.js`) and instanced geometry (`instancing.js`): stone slabs on every walking surface, stacked wall stones, kawara pan tiles with caps, ridges, rafters and boards on every roof, board walls, koshi lattices, shoji, the balcony rail, doors, lanterns, the lamp post and a cloth noren. Cell distance 0.0941 to 0.0899, SSIM 0.2950 to 0.3273, 203 draw calls, 1.0 to 1.5 ms median on the GPU. Three critic rounds: the first found the left doors, a lantern and the awning built inside their walls, a floating sign, three side-stair treads buried in the planter, wall stones half a block proud of their line and a black block on house 1's roof; fixing those first cost 0.004 of cell distance until a bisect showed which fixes the photo disliked. The second found the sign still floating (its ray moved when the wall was set back), the awning sheet under the tile crests, the fence roof's return pieces not meeting, and a wedge of exposed walkway inside the wall notch. The third found the fence roof's corner clipping done on both diagonals (a bowtie) and the notch stones hidden behind a flush band. Two things proved wrong on the way: the right eave descends along the street, and a cross-product normal flips for roofs sloping toward +x; see `detailed/2026-09-05-phase-2.md`.
