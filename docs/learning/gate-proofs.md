# Gate proofs

Each retired lesson's gate is listed with the mutation that made it go red, the failure it produced, and where the evidence lived before retirement. A gate that was never seen red proves nothing.

## clearance, check 1: nothing hangs at head height (2026-09-10)

- Claim (in the gate's own header, `tools/clearance.js`): no canopy geometry hangs lower than 1.90 m — a standing eye of 1.70 m plus 0.20 m — over the walkable street or over the right terrace, and the cherry's low wood is all its own foot rather than a limb at head height, over either surface. Every other bound on how low a blossom may hang is the photo's blossom mask, and a mask is a 2D silhouette — a strand falling down the photo camera's own ray stays inside it the whole way to the paving — so no scored gate can tell a blossom four metres up from one at head height. The gate reads every blossom instance matrix, every strand vertex and every trunk vertex out of the built scene and drops a ray onto the paving under each.
- Origin: the first defect of iteration 2's second pass, in `docs/devlog/detailed/2026-09-10-iteration-2.md`. The canopy reached 1.71 m over the landing where a standing eye is 1.70 m, and `npm run views` pose 5 looked into one card 0.26 m away that filled the left third of the frame. `npm test` was green throughout. It was found by the author's own sweep, not by a user, so there is no row for it in `defect-register.md` and there should not be.
- **A1, the rule switched off.** `const HEADROOM = 2.4;` in `src/vegetation.js` set to `0.0`, which makes `overStreet` false for everything and changes nothing else. The canopy came back at 31428 cards and 35048 strand vertices, and 31428 is exactly the pre-fix census iteration 2 recorded (31428 before the rule, 31368 after), so the mutation reproduced the defect and not something else. Exit 1:

```
FAIL lowest canopy geometry over the walkable street: 1.329 m (limit 1.90 m) — cherry blossoms at (-0.46, -2.93, -15.69) over "landing slabs" at y -4.50

FAIL: 1 clearance problem(s):
  54 of 60457 canopy samples over the walkable street hang below 1.90 m, the lowest at 1.329 m. A
  standing eye on this street is 1.70 m over the paving, so the canopy is at head height. The photo's
  blossom mask cannot bound this — it is a 2D silhouette — so the clearance has to be enforced in world
  space where the canopy is built (src/vegetation.js).
```

- **A2 and A3, the dial.** The iteration 2 devlog names `HEADROOM = 2.2` as a dial for buying back SSIM margin, so the limit has to bite before the dial is turned and not only when the rule is switched off. `HEADROOM = 2.25` and `= 2.20` both read `FAIL … 1.799 m (limit 1.90 m)`, exit 1. The boundary was **measured rather than extrapolated**, which matters because two points are not a slope: `2.30` and `2.35` pass at 2.073 m. So the gate bites somewhere in (2.25, 2.30], and turning the dial to 2.2 turns it red.
- **D1 and D3, the terrace**, which the scene's own rule does not cover at all. The cherry's sub-branches made to dive (`new THREE.Vector3(0, 0.15 + rand() * 0.3, 0)` → `(0, -1.2 - rand() * 0.3, 0)`) gives `FAIL lowest canopy geometry over the right terrace: 0.861 m (limit 1.90 m) — cherry blossoms at (0.94, -0.11, -11.96) over "side stair treads"`, exit 1 — **while the street check stays green at 2.045 m**. That is the hole this assertion exists for: the fringe moves sideways off the street and the street rule cannot see it.
- **D2, a limb at head height over the street**, which lives in the `cherry trunk` mesh and is tested by nothing in the scene. One entry of the `ends` table run to a low point over the stairs (`[0.36, 0.3, 15.0]` → `[0.45, 0.90, 8.0]`, which `uvToWorld` puts 1.66 m over the paving) with the mask clip lifted (`clipToMask(full.getSpacedPoints(16), 1) ?? …` → `full.getSpacedPoints(16)`). Two edits, because one alone cannot reach the defect: clipping a sagging limb to the photo's silhouette shortens it instead of lowering it, which is the same blindness this whole gate is about. `FAIL the cherry's low wood over the street is its own foot: 41 of 1610 vertices of "cherry trunk" sit below 1.90 m, spread 9.99 m (limit 2.00 m) from its deepest point at (2.75, -16.39)`, exit 1 — **while the street headroom check stays green at 2.206 m**, because it measures cards and strands and a limb is neither. That run also reds the terrace check at 0.451 m, so it is not an isolating proof of the trunk assertion on its own; D3 below is.
- **D3, a limb at head height over the terrace.** The same two edits with the end aimed over the right walkway (`[0.62, 0.62, 12.0]`, which `uvToWorld` puts at x 1.81, z −11.32). `FAIL the cherry's low wood over the terrace is its own foot: 65 of 1458 vertices … spread 5.71 m (limit 2.00 m)`, **while the same assertion over the street stays green at 0.87 m**, which is the isolating pair D2 lacks.
- Four other mutations were tried for the limbs and did not reach them, and each failure is worth knowing: dropping a limb's end alone (`[0.36, 0.62, 15.0]`) let `clipToMask` truncate the limb instead; sagging the limbs' control points to −1.2 and to −2.2 bowed them without lowering their ends (`out/scratch/red-P6d`, `red-P6e`); sagging to −4.0 made the page throw. One of them, `red-P6g`, reddened the terrace check at 1.192 m on its way past. The mask cannot be reached around, only lifted.
- Restored (`src/vegetation.js` back to sha256 `939f7e0839e39d8d…`), the same run prints `ok lowest canopy geometry over the walkable street: 2.073 m (limit 1.90 m)`, `ok lowest canopy geometry over the right terrace: 2.526 m`, `ok the cherry's low wood over the street is its own foot: 33 of 1670 vertices … spread 0.87 m`, `ok … over the terrace … 45 of 1318 … spread 0.86 m`, and `clearance headroom: lowest canopy geometry 2.073 m over the walkable street, limit 1.90 m, 60265 samples over 14 paving meshes`.
- The limit is written from the person: a standing eye is 1.70 m over this paving — the eye `npm run views` pose 5 puts there — and 1.90 m is that plus 0.20 m. **The 0.173 m of margin over the shipped 2.073 m is not comfortable and must not be described as an envelope for design change.** The canopy draws every card's size and rotation from one shared PRNG stream, so any edit that changes how many `rand()` calls precede it re-rolls all 31,368 cards: six mutations aimed at the limbs and sub-branches, none of which touched `HEADROOM`, the mask, `streetY` or `streetCenterX`, moved this reading across 2.045 to 2.206 m — a 0.161 m spread, comparable to the margin. The figure reproduces exactly between runs and does not hold still across edits. When an unrelated canopy edit reds this gate, the answer is to look at the frame, not to lower the limit.
- The other thing the margin is spent on is card size, and the number for that is the vertical drop from a card's centre to its lowest corner, which the gate measures and prints: **0.310 m**. (An earlier draft of this entry quoted 0.617 m, which is the widest card's HORIZONTAL extent and sets nothing. An independent critic caught it.)
- The gate reads the BUILT scene and imports no constant from `src/`. That matters here because both the defect and its fix live in exactly the constants a check written from the source would have read.
- Bound: **it covers what the cherry hangs, and only that.** `petals` are excluded, and that is not a convenience: a petal's drawn height is `instanceY - mod(uTime * speed, 7.5)` computed in the vertex shader, so the instance matrix this gate can read is not where a petal is drawn, and petals are built to fall through the street and below it — 182 of the 420 sit over the walkable street at t = 0 on the shipped tree, 68 of those below 1.7 m and the lowest 1.86 m *under* the paving. A matrix-read check on petals would be measuring the wrong number and would fail a correct scene; the drift has to be bounded in the shader if it is ever bounded. A new hanging plant under a name that does not start with `cherry ` is not measured either; every plant the gate does not measure is printed by name every run, which is a weak defence because it prints the names a reader skims. The height comes from CPU matrices, which is where the cards are drawn in y only because the wind's offset has no y term (`vec3(s, 0.0, c * 0.4)`); add one and this gate keeps reporting the still positions. The ground under a card is found on a 0.25 m grid from five points per card, so a card within 0.125 m of the paving's edge can be tested against a neighbouring cell — or, if all five points quantize off the paving, against nothing at all. And the trunk assertion is a *spread*, not a clearance: low wood 1.9 m from the trunk's deepest point passes.

Seven of the 34 are open road. What makes a literal better than the rule it replaced is not the answer, which is identical, but that it cannot be widened by building more: the rule ended the street wherever it was fully closed, so it got more forgiving the more completely a building blocked the road, and one 0.25 m row of block-out silenced the 8 m behind it. The exempt region is now pinned from both sides — no more than 40 rows (34 today) and no blockers in it but those three — so C2 above is red.
- Bound, continued: within the asserted range it measures the paved band only, so a structure standing on ground that is not paving is invisible; and it asserts a clear RUN in each half rather than a clear half, so with bands 4.05 m to 7.00 m wide a structure may occupy several metres of road at any z with every assertion green — the rule is "you can walk down each side", not "the road is empty". A structure is ignored where the air it occupies is entirely under 0.35 m, so a coping or a kerb is stepped over rather than reported (the platform's front coping stands 0.17 m over the platform slabs; the low wall's cap tiles oversail the gutter by 0.69 m and are reported) — and so would a 0.34 m plinth laid across the road. Plants are never blockers, which includes the three pots. Street furniture is: the far half's shipped 1.10 m is the lamp post splitting the run at z -13.75, and against buildings alone the narrowest far-half run is 1.50 m. Every material is forced double-sided for the sweep and restored after, and the air a structure occupies is read per INSTANCE: without the first, a downward ray returns only top faces and a wall running from under the paving to above head height is invisible; without the second, one tile set's high and low pieces merge into one span and the air between them reads as solid. A ZERO-THICKNESS VERTICAL sheet — a screen or a hoarding built as one plane — returns no hit to a downward ray at all and is invisible to both; the scene has one such mesh, `noren`, and it stands off the paved band. And it samples z every 0.25 m and x every 0.05 m, so an obstruction shorter or narrower than that can fall between rays; the defect it exists for is 0.46 m wide and 8 m long.
- **What has NOT been made to go red, in either check: the floors.** They fire only on a scene with a renamed, emptied or deleted set, and no such scene was built. They are: 40,000 canopy samples (shipped 66,284; either canopy mesh disappearing leaves 34,916 or 31,368), 1,000 of them over walkable paving (60,265), a `cherry trunk` mesh existing at all, terrace paving existing at all with a canopy sample over it, 50 structure meshes (176), a structure reaching the paving at a fifth of the street's z positions (131 of 166), walkable paving under 90% of the sampled z positions between the swept window's top and z -33.5 (166 of 166), and no more than 40 paved rows past that bound (34). Their calibration is argued from the shipped figures and nothing else. A partial rename is what happens in practice and some of them would not catch one: renaming `landing` and `landing slabs` together drops every canopy sample over the landing, and both canopy floors count geometry rather than ground, so the gate would report a higher "lowest" and pass.

## npm test: every gate proves it ran (2026-09-10)

- Claim (in the gate's own header, `tools/test.js`): a tool that exits 0 having done nothing is indistinguishable here from a tool that passed, so the `GATES` table pairs each gate with a line that only its finished success path prints (seven gates when this was written; eight since `clearance` landed on 2026-09-10, which brings two markers of its own because it runs two independent checks). `npm test` captures each tool's output and fails, naming the tool and the missing text, when that line is absent — whatever the exit status says.
- Origin: `blackframe`, `record`, `paintcheck` and `shimmer` guarded their main block with ``import.meta.url === `file:///${process.argv[1].replace(/\\/g, '/')}` ``. That is true on Windows and false on every POSIX `argv[1]`: `file:///` joined to `/home/runner/work/scenes/scenes/tools/blackframe.js` gives `file:////home/...` with four slashes, against node's `file:///home/...`. So on CI each tool imported its own module, ran nothing and exited 0. `blackframe` and `record` are in `npm test`, and CI reported them green from the day each landed, 2026-09-06, until 2026-09-09. In run 34306122575, job 102323064914: `== tools/blackframe.js ==` at 03:57:12.599, `== tools/record.js ==` at 03:57:12.893, `== thresholds ==` at 03:57:13.191, `PASS` on the same millisecond. The two gates together took 0.6 s of a 45m38s run, printing nothing; on this machine's GPU they take 19 s and 18 s, and on the runner's SwiftShader they take minutes.
- Mutation: the pre-fix guard put back in `tools/blackframe.js`. The old expression is true on Windows, so reproducing the defect here meant feeding it the `argv[1]` a Linux runner has — `isMainModule` became `const runnerArgv1 = process.argv[1].replace(/^[A-Za-z]:/, '').replace(/\\/g, '/');` followed by the old comparison against `` `file:///${runnerArgv1}` ``. Run on its own, `node tools/blackframe.js` then printed nothing and exited 0 — the CI defect exactly. Then `node tools/test.js` from a clean `out/`.
- Failure produced: exit 1 at the sixth gate, after shot (58 s), compare (1 s), placement (35 s), animation (998 s) and nudge (164 s) had passed and printed their markers:

```
== tools/blackframe.js ==

FAIL: tools/blackframe.js exited 0 after 0 s without producing evidence that it ran.
  expected its summary line to contain: "blackframe:"
  it printed 0 character(s) of output (nothing at all)
  A gate that exits 0 having run nothing is reported here as a pass, so the marker is
  the evidence. Check the main-module guard at the bottom of the tool first: the form
  `import.meta.url === `file:///${process.argv[1]...}`` is false on every POSIX argv[1]
  and silently skips the whole main block. Fix the tool, or, if it now prints a
```

- Restored, the same suite from a clean `out/` printed `blackframe: 12 size/ratio combinations from 6 of 6 views all rendered the scene on ANGLE (NVIDIA, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 ...)`, then `-- tools/blackframe.js: ok in 19 s`, then `record: 847 frames driven by real input over 12 s ..., worst frame 5 of 25 probes dark (limit 12)` and `PASS`. Seven gates, exit 0: shot 63 s, compare 1 s, placement 30 s, animation 811 s, nudge 163 s, blackframe 19 s, record 18 s.
- The guard itself is checked separately, because a truth table is not a gate: evaluating both forms against a Windows-shaped and a POSIX-shaped `argv[1]` with node's own `path.win32`/`path.posix` and `fileURLToPath(url, { windows })` gives old true/false and new true/true. That is what the fix rests on; the marker check is what notices when a future guard, or anything else, silences a tool again.
- Bound: **it proves a marker was printed, not that the gate's checks are right.** A tool that printed its summary line and skipped half its work passes this, and the marker is a plain substring, so a tool that renames its summary line goes red until `GATES` is updated. It covers the tools in `GATES` and nothing else — `paintcheck` and `shimmer` are not in `npm test`, so their identical guards are covered by the same fix and by nothing that runs. `record` under `RECORD=0` prints `record: skipped by RECORD=0 ...`, which satisfies the marker on purpose: the check separates a decision in the log from silence, not a run from a skip.

## blackframe: the frame is there, at every window size (2026-09-06)

- Claim (in the gate's own header, `tools/blackframe.js`): every gate in the repo runs at one viewport and device pixel ratio, and the post chain can hand back a black frame from the *size* alone. For six window size and ratio combinations, including non-round ratios, measured once on load and once after a resize, the gate fails when the frame is more than 20% near black, when one tile of a 12x12 grid over it is essentially all black, when its luminance is flat, or when the composer has kept less than 35% of the light the same view shows through `renderer.render(scene, camera)` with no post chain at all.
- Origin: the user's report, "when I move the camera around there are even giant rectangular blackouts on screen... Only clicking the reset review button made it go away", recorded in `defect-register.md` with the investigation that found the camera incidental and the frame full of NaN.
- Mutation: `src/post.js` and `src/main.js` reverted to their state at 0c828d7, which is the post chain with no verification of its own output, then `npm run blackframe`.
- Failure produced: 4 of 12 rows, exit status 1. At the size the report came from, a 1280x720 window at a device pixel ratio of 1.5 and so a 1920x1080 drawing buffer: `96.61% of the frame is near black, over the 20% allowed`, `a 160x90 tile (one of a 12x12 grid over the frame) is 100% near black`, and `the composer keeps only 3.1% of the light the plain renderer sees (3.6 against 116.1)`. Also 1441x801 at ratio 1.25, reached by a resize, at 100% near black with a luminance spread of 0 and none of the light kept; 1575x1435 at ratio 1.75 at 95.68%; and a resize back onto a 1920x1080 buffer at ratio 2, again 96.61%. Restored, the same run printed `blackframe: 12 size/ratio combinations all rendered the scene`, with the frames 0.56% to 1.70% near black, the worst tile 7.6% to 16.1%, and 1.009 to 1.022 of the plain renderer's light kept.
- The limits sit far from both states on every one of the four measures, which is what a gate for "rendered nothing" should look like: 1.7% against 96.6% of near-black pixels, 16% against 100% on the worst tile, a luminance spread of 57 against 0, and 1.01 against 0.03 of the light kept. The differential against the plain renderer is the one that needs no absolute brightness and so holds wherever the camera is pointing when a window is resized.
- A first version used a fixed 32x32 tile, copied from `tools/blackout.js`, and went red against the *fixed* scene: at a 1920x1080 buffer the darkest 32x32 patch of this back-lit sunset is 99.9% near black all on its own, under the eaves. The tile is a twelfth of the frame in each direction now, which is the scale the user's word "giant" is about, and a correct frame reaches 16% of it. `tools/blackout.js` still uses the 32x32 form and would false-positive the same way outside its own viewport.
- Bound: it runs on whatever renderer chromium gives it. With `--use-angle=d3d11` that is the real GPU, and the defect is a property of that driver, so a machine with no usable GPU falls back to SwiftShader and a green run there proves only that the chain is sound there; the renderer is printed with every run so the two are never confused. `BLACKFRAME_VIEWS` trims the sweep for CI, which has no GPU and no time. It looks for frames that are too dark or too flat, so a defect that paints a bright uniform rectangle over part of the frame is invisible to it. And it visits six sizes rather than all of them, which is exactly why the fix it guards is a runtime check rather than a list: no list of sizes can be complete.

## nudge: the frame is stable under a small camera move (2026-09-06)

- Claim (in the gate's own header, `tools/nudge.js`): a still camera renders the same frame byte for byte, so no scored gate can see a frame that is unstable while the camera moves. For three poses at two device pixel ratios the gate renders the same pose twice and requires identical output, then nudges the camera 2 mm and requires the fraction of pixels changing by more than 90 levels to stay under its per-ratio limits (0.4% per pose and 0.2% on the mean at ratio 1; 0.6% and 0.3% at ratio 2).
- Origin: the user's report, "As I move the camera around it flickers a lot", recorded in `defect-register.md` with the investigation that traced it to geometry thinner than a pixel rather than to depth fighting.
- Mutation: `POST.renderScale` set back to 1.0 and `POST.samples` to 4 in `src/post.js`, which is the state the user saw, then `npm run nudge`.
- Failure produced: four failures, exit status 1. `FAIL close to the paving, ratio 1: 0.57% ... over the 0.4% allowed`, the same pose at ratio 2 with 0.70% over 0.6%, and both means (0.298 over 0.2 at ratio 1, 0.369 over 0.3 at ratio 2). Restored, the same run printed `nudge: 6 poses stable across device pixel ratios 1 and 2`.
- The limits sit between the two measured states at each ratio, roughly 1.3x above the fixed scene and 1.3x below the broken one. The mean does the separating and the per-pose ceiling catches a single bad view that a mean would dilute.
- The first version of the gate ran only at ratio 1, and it passed a fix that was switched off at ratio 2 and rendering the scene at a quarter of the canvas there. That is why the gate runs both.
- Bound: three poses, one nudge direction, one viewport (900x820) and one renderer. It measures the whole frame, so a small patch of violent instability can hide under the fraction. It counts only pixels that change *drastically*, so it says nothing about the ordinary resampling a moving camera always produces. And it has about one bit of resolution on the supersample: 1.2, 1.35, 1.5 and 2.0 all pass alike, and only turning it off fails, so it proves the class rather than the setting.
- The bound that mattered, found on 2026-09-06: this gate scores how much the frame *changes*, so a frame that is entirely black is the most stable frame there is and passes it perfectly. It rated the next defect the same user reported, a post chain handing back 96.6% black at a size this gate never visits, as a flawless pass for its whole life. A stability metric cannot tell "rendered correctly" from "rendered nothing", and `npm run blackframe` is the gate that can.

## animation: the scene moves, and not too much (2026-09-05)

- Claim (in the gate's own header, `tools/animation.js`): the scored shot is one frame, so it cannot see what the animation does at t = 3 s. The gate scores seven frames across the wind's slowest period and asserts the worst frame's scores, the spread between frames, and the mean per-pixel change between neighbouring frames from both sides: too little and the scene has stopped moving, too much and it is thrashing.
