# Defect register

Every defect the user reports is recorded here and gated, never only fixed. The entry stays after it becomes a gate: this is the standing list of what the gates could not see.

Entry shape: date, symptom as the user saw it, investigation, root cause, and how it is checked from now on (the gate covers the defect's whole class, not the one instance).

| 2026-09-06 | "when I move the camera around there are even giant rectangular blackouts on screen. It continues even after the camera stops moving. Only clicking the reset review button made it go away." | The camera is incidental: this reproduces with the camera stationary, from the size of the composer's targets alone, and the reset only ever appeared to help because whatever else changed alongside it changed the size. Measured on ANGLE/D3D11 (NVIDIA RTX 4090) at a 1280x720 window and a device pixel ratio of 1.5, so a 1920x1080 drawing buffer and a 2304x1296 composer: 96.6% of the frame is near black through the composer against 0.8% through `renderer.render(scene, camera)`. The frame is not black, it is **NaN**: read back as half floats, both of the composer's ping-pong targets are 100% NaN, and NaN reaches the canvas as black. Sweeping the composer's size from 1.00 to 1.60 times the drawing buffer in hundredths, 14 of the 61 sizes fail at 8 samples and 10 of 61 at 4, and the two sets only partly overlap; every size is deterministic to two decimal places over repeats and arriving from either direction, with `gl.getError()` 0, no context loss and FRAMEBUFFER_COMPLETE throughout. Turning the bloom pass off makes every bad size correct (1.96%); setting its strength to 0, its threshold to 1e6 or its radius to 0 does not, so the bloom spreads the NaN rather than making it. Setting the composer's targets to 0 samples makes every bad size correct. The scene is innocent: rendered into a fresh target of the same size, multisampled or not, it reads back with no NaN at all and a peak radiance of 1.65. Two explanations were tested and killed. A broken multisample *resolve*: a bright gradient painted through the very same target and sampled back comes back perfect at every bad size, and so does the whole chain run on a synthetic input, so only the real scene triggers it and no synthetic probe can stand in for the frame. And a buffer that could be routed around: giving the scene render its own multisampled target and leaving the composer's own buffers unmultisampled still produced the NaN, even though that scene target itself read back clean. | A multisampled half-float render target of certain sizes poisons the post chain on this driver, and the bloom's blur carries the resulting NaN across the whole frame. It is a property of the (size, sample count) pair and of nothing in the scene, no API call admits to it, and there is no rule about the sizes to be had: of every size measured, 2304x1296 is the only one whose width and height are both multiples of 16, so an alignment rule would steer toward the failures rather than away. | `npm run blackframe` (in `npm test`): six window size and device pixel ratio combinations including non-round ratios, each measured on load and again after a resize, failing when the frame is too dark, too flat, has a blacked-out tile of a 12x12 grid, or has lost most of the light the plain renderer sees. The fix is in `src/post.js`: on build and on every size change the chain renders one real frame, samples both of its buffers for anything that is not a finite number, weighs its brightness against the same scene rendered at 64x64 with no post chain, and steps down a ladder of configurations if the frame is not there, starting again from the top at the next size. Proof of the red run in `gate-proofs.md`. Note what did *not* see this: `npm run nudge`, the gate written for the previous report from the same user, scores the fraction of pixels that *change* between two renders of almost the same view. A frame that is entirely black is the most stable frame there is, so nudge scored this catastrophe as a flawless pass for its whole life. A stability metric cannot tell "rendered correctly" from "rendered nothing". |
| 2026-09-06 | "As I move the camera around it flickers a lot" | Two renders of an identical pose are byte-identical, so nothing is nondeterministic per frame and the animation is not involved. A 2 mm camera nudge changed 4.76% of pixels at the photo view (0.32% of them by more than 90 levels), 5.39% close to the paving (0.57%), 0.19% orbited (0.01%). The first hypothesis was depth fighting between the surfaces phase 2 stacked centimetres apart, and it was wrong: the composer's render targets carry 24-bit depth (measured, not assumed), and moving the camera's near from 0.2 to 1.0 or 2.0, or far from 6000 to 400, changed the photo view's figure not at all. Disabling bloom, and the whole post chain, changed nothing either. An amplified difference image settled it: the change sits on the edges of fine geometry and all through the canopy, with flat interiors untouched, which is not what a depth fight looks like. Then the remedies were measured: maximum anisotropy did nothing, alpha-to-coverage did nothing, and supersampling halved the drastic changes. Asking for eight multisamples produced byte-identical output, which was first written down as "SwiftShader does not honour the request"; the critic showed that was wrong. SwiftShader reports a maximum of four samples and WebGL clamps to it, so the request worked and simply could not go higher there; a desktop GPU reports eight and does use them. Multisampling turned out to be carrying more than half the stability, and switching it off at the shipped supersample doubles the drastic changes. The first fix was also switched off exactly where it was needed most: it capped its own scale against the device pixel ratio and sized its target from CSS pixels, so at a pixel ratio of 2 the scene rendered at a quarter of the canvas and scaled up, measuring worse than the state the user complained about, while the gate ran only at ratio 1 and saw nothing. | Geometric aliasing, not depth. The scene is full of geometry thinner than a pixel: the cherry's strand tubes are about 0.35 px wide at their distance, and so are tile ridges, lattice slats and the edges of some thirty thousand alpha-tested blossom cards. A triangle that lands between the samples either covers one or covers none, so a fraction-of-a-pixel camera move switches whole pixels on and off. Multisampling catches most of it and supersampling catches more; neither can help what falls between all of the samples. | `npm run nudge` (in `npm test`): three poses at two device pixel ratios, each rendered twice for an identical-frame check and then with the camera moved 2 mm, failing when too many pixels change by more than 90 levels. The limits are per ratio, because a ratio 2 pixel covers a quarter of the screen area and the fractions are not comparable. The fix is in `src/post.js`: eight requested multisamples (the driver clamps to its own maximum) and `POST.renderScale` at 1.2, so the scene renders above the drawing buffer and resolves down. Measured, fixed against the state the user saw: at ratio 1 the three poses go 0.15/0.00/0.28 against 0.32/0.01/0.57, and at ratio 2 0.26/0.00/0.50 against 0.40/0.00/0.70. Costs 0.6 ms of a 16 ms budget. Proof of the red run in `gate-proofs.md`. |
